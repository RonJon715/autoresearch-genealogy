## Biography

According to Edward Hasted, *The History and Topographical Survey of the County of Kent*, vol. 5 (1798), pp. 375-385, and corroborated by genealogical records on WikiTree (Filmer-6) and published in James M. Allen Jr., *Major Henry Filmer of England and Virginia: A Partial List of Descendants* (2005):

Major Henry Filmer was one of Sir Edward Filmer's eighteen children (nine sons and nine daughters) by his wife Dame Elizabeth Argall. The Filmer family was originally seated at the manor of Herst in the parish of Otterden, Kent, where Robert Filmer lived in King Edward II's reign. His descendants continued there until Robert Filmer, son of James, removed to the manor of Little Charlton in East Sutton parish. (Strong Signal; Hasted's Kent, published parish history)

Sir Edward Filmer purchased the manor of East Sutton from his brother in law John Argall, and removed from Little Charlton to the manor house of East Sutton, called East Sutton Place, where he kept his shrievalty in the 13th year of James I's reign. On July 23, 1603, Sir Edward was knighted by King James I. He was appointed High Sheriff of Kent in 1614. (Strong Signal; Hasted's Kent)

Henry emigrated to Virginia and settled first at James City. He served as Burgess for James City County 1642 to 1643 and as Justice of Warwick County in 1647. He named his plantation on Mulberry Island "Laus Deo." (Moderate Signal; Allen 2005, secondary source)
