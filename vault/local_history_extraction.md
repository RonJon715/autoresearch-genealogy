## Cluster 1: Liebling, Banat (Donauschwaben)

Target families: Hedrich, Koller, Kind.

### Sources Found

**1. Ortssippenbuch Liebling im Banat**
- Author: Johann Moehler
- Year: 1979
- Location: Published in Altshausen, Germany
- Pages: 836
- Coverage: 6,303 families from Liebling
- URL: [GenWiki OFB entry](https://wiki.genealogy.net/Liebling_1979,_OFB)
- Relevance: Strong Signal. This is a comprehensive parish family register covering the entire village. Hedrich, Koller, and Kind families would likely appear if they lived in Liebling during the periods covered.

**2. Lieblinger Familienbuch (1994 edition)**
- Year: 1994
- Location: Published in Ludwigshafen am Rhein, Germany
- URL: [GenWiki OFB entry](https://wiki.genealogy.net/Liebling_1994,_OFB)
- Coverage: Updated or supplemental family book for Liebling.
- Relevance: Strong Signal. A later edition may include corrections or additions to the 1979 volume.

**3. Lieblinger Familienbuch (CD edition)**
- Author: Adam Arnold
- Year: 2001
- Format: CD-ROM
- URL: [WeRelate source page](https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Source:Arnold,_Adam._Lieblinger_Familienbuch)
- Coverage: Digital version of Liebling family records. Available through Family History Centers.
- Relevance: Strong Signal. Digital format may be easier to search for specific surnames.

**4. Banat Books (AVBF Familienbuch collection)**
- URL: [banatbooks.com Familienbuch list](https://www.banatbooks.com/FamilienbuchList.htm)
- URL: [banatbooks.com CD ordering](https://www.banatbooks.com/CDs.htm)
- Coverage: Clearinghouse for Banat village family books, including Liebling volumes.
- Relevance: Moderate Signal. Distribution channel for acquiring the above sources.

**5. Lieblinger Verein Chicago**
- URL: [lieblingerchicago.com](https://www.lieblingerchicago.com/)
- Coverage: Emigrant association for Liebling descendants in the United States. Maintains historical photos and community records.
- Relevance: Moderate Signal. May hold oral histories, photographs, or unpublished family data for Hedrich, Koller, and Kind families.

**6. DVHH (Donauschwaben Villages Helping Hands)**
- URL: [dvhh.org main](https://main.dvhh.org/)
- URL: [DVHH genealogy volunteer lookups](https://dvhh.org/genealogy/2.lookup-books.htm)
- URL: [DVHH emigrant reference books](https://dvhh.org/genealogy/3.emigrant_ref_books.htm)
- Coverage: Volunteer lookup service for Donauschwaben village family books. Maintains a contact registry and lookup guide system organized by village.
- Relevance: Moderate Signal. The DVHH lookup service can request searches in the Liebling Familienbuch volumes for specific surnames (Hedrich, Koller, Kind). The Schowe resources page references surname lists that include Hedrich and Koller variants (Goller).

### Facts Extracted

- Three separate Liebling Familienbuch editions exist (1979, 1994, 2001 CD), covering over 6,300 families.
- The Lieblinger Verein Chicago is an active emigrant association that may hold supplementary records.
- DVHH offers volunteer lookups in village family books, providing a path to extract specific entries for Hedrich, Koller, and Kind without acquiring the full volumes.
- The surname Koller may appear as "Goller" in some Banat records (noted in Schowe resources on dvhh.org).

### Negative Results

- Searched for "Lieblinger Heimatbuch" (a published hometown book): no publication with that exact title was found. The Lieblinger Familienbuch editions appear to be the primary published works. No standalone Heimatbuch for Liebling was identified.
- No direct DVHH page specifically dedicated to Liebling village was found in search results, though DVHH covers Banat villages broadly.
- No online digitized version of any Liebling Familienbuch was found; access appears to require CD purchase or Family History Center visit.

## Cluster 2: Grayson County, Virginia

Target families: Hale, Bourne.

### Sources Found

**1. Pioneer Settlers of Grayson County, Virginia**
- Author: Benjamin Floyd Nuckolls (b. 1838)
- Year: 1914 (original); reprinted 2011 with index
- Publisher: The King Printing Company, Bristol, Tennessee
- Pages: 290 (2011 reprint)
- Coverage: Approximately 4,000 individuals. Covers pioneer families including progenitors, descendants, and allied families through marriage across several generations.
- URL: [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/details/pioneersettlerso00nuck)
- URL: [Library of Congress catalog](https://www.loc.gov/item/15019440/)
- URL: [New River Notes digitized chapters](https://www.newrivernotes.com/pioneer-settlers-of-grayson-county-virginia/)
- URL: [Heritage Books reprint](https://heritagebooks.com/products/102-4215)
- Relevance: Strong Signal. Both Hale and Bourne are explicitly listed among the principal families treated in this volume.

### Facts Extracted

- The Nuckolls book explicitly covers both the Hale and Bourne families of Grayson County (source: Amazon and Heritage Books descriptions listing principal families).
- Grayson County was formed in 1792 from portions of Patrick and Wythe Counties, located near the North Carolina and Tennessee borders.
- The book is freely available in full text on the Internet Archive, making extraction straightforward.
- Principal families listed include (among others): Anderson, Blair, Bourne, Bryan, Coltrane, Cornett, Cox, Davis, Hale, Hanks, Hash, Jones, Nuckolls, Osborne, Perkins, Vaughn, and Ward. Many of these surnames appear in this vault.
- The New River Notes website has digitized portions of the text, which may be searchable online.

### Negative Results

- None for this search. The target source was located with full text access confirmed.

**7. Early Colonial Settlers of Southern Maryland and Virginia's Northern Neck Counties**
- URL: [colonial-settlers-md-va.us](https://www.colonial-settlers-md-va.us/)
- Coverage: Database of early colonial settlers in the Maryland/Virginia border region.
- Relevance: Moderate Signal. Northern Neck is relevant geography for Faulconer family origins.

### Facts Extracted

- Thomas Faulconer (also spelled Faulkner) sailed to Virginia Colony in 1622 on the Mary Providence as an indentured servant sponsored by Robert Salford, landing at Elizabeth City (now Hampton, Virginia).
- In 1639, Faulconer sponsored passage for five people from England (including his wife), receiving 250 acres of headright land.
- Thomas Faulconer served as the first Church of England minister for St. Luke's Church in Smithfield, Virginia, and was the third and sole minister of Warwisqueake Parish in Isle of Wight County in 1642.
- William Faulconer (b. 1724, d. May 28, 1778) lived in Orange (later Montgomery) County, St. Thomas Parish, Virginia.
- John Faulconer and Joyce Craig moved from Essex County, Virginia, into the Bluegrass region of central Kentucky in 1779 via the Wilderness Road.
- The Faulkner/Faulconer spelling variants have been used inconsistently across generations. Other variants in the vault include: Falconer, Falkner, Falkiner, Forkner, Fortner, Fowkner.

### Negative Results

- No published, dedicated Faulkner/Faulconer family genealogy book was identified in search results. The information available is primarily from collaborative genealogy platforms (WikiTree, Geni) and forum discussions rather than a peer reviewed or professionally published work.
- The Ancestry "Maryland and Virginia Colonials" collection was identified but could not be accessed to confirm Faulkner/Faulconer inclusion.
