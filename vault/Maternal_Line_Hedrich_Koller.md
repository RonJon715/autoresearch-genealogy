# Maternal Line: Hedrich, Koller, and Kind Families

Generation by generation descent on the maternal side of Ronald Ray Faulkner Jr. Three intermarried Donauschwaben (Danube Swabian) families from the village of Liebling, Timis County, Romania (formerly Hungary), who immigrated to Chicago as post-WWII displaced persons.

## Migration Path

**Liebling, Banat, Hungary/Romania (1850s)** > **Displaced persons camps, Austria (1944-1950)** > **Chicago, Cook County, Illinois (c.1950)**

---

## The Connection

Ronald Ray Faulkner Jr's mother is **[[Kathrine_A_Faulkner]]** (nee Hedrich), born 13 January 1960 in Chicago. She is the daughter of Friedrich Hedrich and Maria Koller, both born in Liebling, Romania.

---

## HEDRICH LINE (Paternal Grandmother's Line)

### Martin Hedrich (1846-1910)
**Liebling, Timis, Hungary**

Earliest documented Hedrich ancestor. Born 5 November 1846 in Liebling. Married **[[Katharina_Schäfer]]** after 15 May 1879 in Liebling. Died 5 September 1910.

The Hedrich family is documented in two published Ortsfamilienbücher (parish family books) for Liebling:
1. Johann Mohler, *Ortssippenbuch Liebling im Banat* (Altshausen, 1979), 836 pages, 6,303 families. (Strong Signal)
2. Adam Arnold, *Lieblinger Familienbuch* (1994; 2001 CD-ROM edition). (Strong Signal)

The Hedrich surname also appears in David Dreyer's Banat Ship List database, documenting emigration from Liebling to Chicago, Harrisburg (PA), and Winnipeg.

Sources: Ancestry Family Trees; Mohler Ortssippenbuch; Dreyer Database. Confidence: Moderate.

### Philippina Hedrich (1906-1995)
**Liebling, Romania > Chicago, Illinois**

Born 24 September 1906 in Liebling. Daughter of Martin Hedrich (1882) and Maria Barbara Kind. Married **[[Friedrich_Hedrich_1903]]** (Friedrich Hedrich, b. 19 July 1903, Liebling; d. 24 July 1981).

**Immigration:** Emigrated from the Romanian Banat to the United States as a post-WWII displaced person, arriving c.1950. The Donauschwaben were displaced beginning in 1944 during the Soviet advance. Settled in Chicago, where a large Donauschwaben community existed, centered around organizations like the Lieblinger Verein Chicago.

Died 30 July 1995 in Chicago, age 88. Obituary published in the Chicago Tribune, 1 August 1995: "Devoted wife of the late Frederich, beloved mother of Fred (Maria) and Katherina (Stefan) Just." Visitation at Drake Son Funeral Home, 5303 N. Western, Chicago. Interment at Rosehill Cemetery.

Sources: Chicago Tribune obituary (1 Aug 1995); Ancestry Family Trees; Social Security Applications Index. Confidence: High.

## KOLLER LINE (Maternal Grandmother's Line)

### Johann Koller (1863-1949)
**Liebling, Timis, Hungary**

Earliest documented Koller ancestor. Born 24 November 1863 in Liebling. Married **[[Elisabetha_Roth]]**. Died 11 September 1949.

Six children documented: [[Maria_Koller_1892]] (1892, died 1916), [[Margaretha_Koller]] (1894), [[Johann_Koller_1896]] (1896), [[Elisabetha_Koller]] (1898), [[Peter_Koller]] (1900), [[Georg_Koller]] (1902).

The Koller family is documented in the same Liebling Ortsfamilienbücher as the Hedrich family. Dreyer's Banat Ship List shows Koller family members emigrating from Liebling, including one going to "cousin Andreas Koller" in Chicago in 1912, establishing the chain migration network.

Sources: Ancestry Family Trees; Mohler Ortssippenbuch (not yet directly consulted); Dreyer Database. Confidence: Moderate.

---

### Georg Koller (1902-1949)
**Liebling, Timis, Hungary**

Born 1902 in Liebling. Son of Johann Koller (1863) and Elisabetha Roth. Married **[[Elisabetha_Koller_1906]]** (b. 19 September 1906, Liebling; d. 1993).

**Georg died in 1949**, before the main wave of Donauschwaben DP immigration (c.1950). It is unclear whether he died in Liebling, in a displaced persons camp, or after emigrating. His death left Elisabetha as a widow with young daughters.

Children: [[Elisabetha_Koller_1927]] (1927), [[Maria_Koller]] (1929).

Sources: Ancestry Family Trees. Confidence: High.

---

### Maria Koller (1929-2018)
**Liebling, Romania > Chicago, Illinois**

Born 2 April 1929 in Liebling. Daughter of Georg Koller (1902) and Elisabetha Koller (1906). Her father died in 1949; her mother survived to 1993.

**Immigration:** Emigrated from the Romanian Banat as a post-WWII displaced person, arriving c.1950 in Chicago. Naturalization: Northern District, Illinois Naturalization Index 1926-1979; Illinois Federal Naturalization Records 1856-1991 (Ancestry hints, not yet verified).

Married **[[Friedrich_Hedrich_1927]]** (Friedrich Hedrich, b. 28 April 1927, Liebling). Married 69 years.

Died 31 October 2018 in Chicago. Buried in Chicago, Cook County.

**This is the user's maternal grandmother.**

Sources: Dignity Memorial obituary (Fredrich Hedrich); Find A Grave Index; Illinois Naturalization records (Ancestry hints). Confidence: High.

## Key Research Gaps

1. **No passenger manifests found** for any of the c.1950 displaced persons arrivals. The naturalization petitions (National Archives at Chicago, RG 21) would contain ship names and arrival dates. These are the single highest value records to retrieve.
2. **Georg Koller's death circumstances** (1949): died before or during the displacement? Location unknown.
3. **Wilhelm Kind's parents**: Listed as open question in Open_Questions.md. The Mohler Ortssippenbuch (1979) would almost certainly contain this information.
4. **Maria Barbara Kind's birth and death dates**: No dates recorded.
5. **Friedrich Hedrich (1903) parents**: Unknown in GEDCOM.
6. **Elisabetha Roth** (wife of Johann Koller): No dates or parentage recorded.
7. **Christina Ohlhausen's parents**: Unknown.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Order the Mohler Ortssippenbuch** (1979) or the Arnold Lieblinger Familienbuch (2001 CD). These books contain 6,303 families and would fill in the Kind, Hedrich, Koller, and Ohlhausen parentage going back to the 18th century Banat colonization.
2. **Request naturalization petitions** from the National Archives at Chicago for Friedrich Hedrich (1927) and Maria Koller (1929). These contain ship name, arrival date, and exact birthplace.
3. **Contact the Lieblinger Verein Chicago** for community records and oral history.
4. **File USCIS Genealogy Program requests** ($65/search) for A-Files (alien registration files) which contain immigration details.
5. **Search Arolsen Archives** (formerly International Tracing Service) for displaced persons camp registration cards.
