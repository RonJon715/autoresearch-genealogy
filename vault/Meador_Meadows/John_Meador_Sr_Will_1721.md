# Will of John Meador (Meadows) Sr. (1721)

## Source

Will proved in Essex County, Virginia, ca. November 1721. John Meador died ca. 21 November 1721 in Essex County (formerly Old Rappahannock County), Virginia.

## Summary

John Meador Sr. had two wives and thirteen children. Both wives and four children predeceased him and were not named in the will. The will divided his 450-acre property (originally granted to his father Thomas Meador as an orphan) among the children of his first wife. Portions were also allocated to the children of his second wife.

**Daughter Dinah**: Given only one shilling, "apparently because of her husband."

## Marriages

1. **Elizabeth White** (m. ca. 1677/78, Essex County, VA). Daughter of Richard White and Addra. Elizabeth born 1659, died 17 August 1694, Essex County.
2. **Mary Awbrey** (m. after 1694). Daughter of Richard Awbrey and Dorothy North.

## Related Probate: John Meador Jr. (son)

On 18 May 1720, the Nuncupative (verbal) will of John Meador Jr. was presented in court by William Bourne and Thomas Meador, his executors. Proved by oaths of John Allen, Richard Grigsby, and Ann Bradbury. John Meador Jr. died 14 May 1720, leaving his estate to daughter Susannah Meador. If Susannah died before majority, the estate would be divided between William Bourne, Thomas Meador, John Armstrong, and William Jordan.

This connection between the Meador and Bourne families is significant, as William Bourne served as executor.

## Land Records

John Meador Sr. held 50 acres on the south side of the Rappahannock River, adjoining his own and Henry Awbrey's land, in Old Rappahannock County.

Descendant William Meador's deeds in 1728 and 1734 traced the lineage from great grandfather Thomas Meador through John, Richard, and William, making the descent of property and lineage "unquestionable."

## Calendar Note

Old Rappahannock County was divided in 1692 into Richmond County and Essex County. Records for the Meador family are found under both Old Rappahannock and Essex County designations.
