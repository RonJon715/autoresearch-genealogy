# Colonial Records Search, Pass 2

Follow up search for colonial records on five ancestors not fully covered in pass 1. Conducted 2026-04-06. Goal: locate additional land grants, militia records, probate records, and court records beyond what was already documented.

---

## 1. Major Andrew Monroe (c.1630, 1668), Westmoreland County, Virginia

Father of William Monroe (1666, 1737). Fought at the Battle of Preston on 17 August 1648 under General Sir George Munro, taken prisoner, and banished to Virginia. The 1650 Northumberland County land patent (200 acres on Monroe Creek) was already documented in pass 1.

### Records Found

**Vestry election, July 1661.** Andrew Monroe was elected to the Vestry of Appomattox Parish, Westmoreland County, Virginia. This places him as an active, propertied member of the parish establishment by 1661. Source: [Genealogie Online, Maj. Andrew Monroe](https://www.genealogieonline.nl/en/genealogie-richard-remme/I138570.php).

**Marriage to Elizabeth Alexander.** Andrew Monroe married Elizabeth, described as the daughter of Colonel John Alexander. The marriage date is not recorded, but it predates the birth of their son William Monroe in 1666. Source: [Genealogie Online, Maj. Andrew Monroe](https://www.genealogieonline.nl/en/genealogie-richard-remme/I138570.php); [WikiTree, Andrew Monroe](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Monroe-377).

**Death in 1668 and widow's subsequent marriages.** Andrew Monroe died in Westmoreland County in 1668. His widow Elizabeth Monroe married secondly George Horner (before 30 July 1679), and thirdly Edward Mountjoy of Westmoreland County (before 23 February 1686/7). These dates come from Westmoreland County court records. Source: [WikiTree, Andrew Monroe](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Monroe-377); [H600 Project, Monroe families of Virginia](https://h600.org/wiki/B09-MonroeOfVirginia).

**Historical dispute on the Battle of Preston connection.** Two competing narratives exist: (1) Andrew Monroe came to Maryland in 1637 and moved to Virginia in 1648, and was not the Major Andrew Munro who fought at Preston; (2) Andrew Monroe came to America c.1642, settled in Maryland, returned to Scotland c.1648, fought at Preston, was captured and banished back to Virginia. The second narrative is the traditional family account. Source: [H600 Project, Monroe families of Virginia](https://h600.org/wiki/B09-MonroeOfVirginia); [WikiTree, Andrew Monroe](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Monroe-377).

### Records Not Found

- No will or estate inventory for Andrew Monroe was located. Westmoreland County records from this period are largely destroyed; most pre-Revolutionary records are missing.
- No additional land grants beyond the 1650 Northumberland County patent were found in online abstracts.
- No militia muster rolls or commission records naming Andrew Monroe were located. Virginia militia records before the French and Indian War (1754) are extremely sparse. The resource "Virginia Colonial Militia 1651, 1776" by William Armstrong Crozier was identified as a potential source but was not searchable online.
- No Westmoreland County court order book entries referencing Andrew Monroe were located in digitized collections.

### Confidence

**Moderate Signal.** The vestry election and widow remarriage dates add new datapoints. However, the absence of a will or estate record, combined with the historiographical dispute about the Battle of Preston connection, means the full biographical outline remains partially speculative. The title "Major" is not confirmed by any surviving military commission document.

## 2. Margaret Bowcock (Monroe), Wife of William Monroe (1666, 1737)

Married William Monroe before 22 March 1689 in the Northern Neck of Virginia (documented in pass 1). Vault file: [[Bowcock]].

### Records Found

**Father identified as Thomas Bowcock (c.1625, after 1687).** Thomas Bowcock was reportedly the first Bowcock to arrive in America, coming by ship in 1646. He was brought by George Ludlow of York, Virginia, and was more than likely an indentured servant. He died after 1687 in Westmoreland, Virginia. Source: [Richard Gwyn Allen Blog, Bocock/Bowcock Family](https://richardgwynallenblog.wordpress.com/category/bocockbowcock-family/).

**Lancashire origins.** The Bowcock surname is associated with Lancashire in northwest England. The family arrived in the American colonies in the mid 17th century. Source: [Richard Gwyn Allen Blog, Bocock/Bowcock Family](https://richardgwynallenblog.wordpress.com/category/bocockbowcock-family/).

**Margaret's dates.** Born c.1670 in Westmoreland, Virginia; died 1737 in Westmoreland, Virginia. Parents listed as Sir Thomas Bowcock and Janet Bowcock (born White) on secondary genealogical sites; the "Sir" title is unverified. Source: [Geni, Margaret Monroe (Bowcock)](https://www.geni.com/people/Margaret-Monroe/6000000008452416544).

**Related Bowcock: Henry Bowcock, Williamsburg tavern keeper.** Henry Bowcock operated an ordinary (tavern) in Williamsburg, renewing his license annually until his death (last license dated 18 August 1729). His widow Mary was granted a license on 20 July 1730 and later married Henry Wetherburn in 1731. The relationship between Henry Bowcock and Thomas Bowcock/Margaret Bowcock is not established in the sources found. Source: [Colonial Williamsburg Research](https://research.colonialwilliamsburg.org/library/materials/topics/seventeenth-century-virginians/).

### Records Not Found

- No Westmoreland County land records, deeds, or court records were found naming Thomas Bowcock or Margaret Bowcock specifically.
- No marriage record for Margaret Bowcock and William Monroe was located (this was noted in pass 1 as well; the marriage is inferred from secondary sources).
- No birth, baptism, or death record for Margaret Bowcock was located in any digitized collection.
- The "Sir Thomas Bowcock" designation and the mother's name "Janet White" appear only on user contributed genealogy sites (Geni, MyHeritage) and lack primary source citations. The "Sir" title for an indentured servant is contradictory and likely erroneous.
- No connection between Henry Bowcock of Williamsburg and Margaret Bowcock of Westmoreland County was established.

### Confidence

**Speculative.** The Bowcock family in colonial Virginia is very poorly documented. The identification of Thomas Bowcock as Margaret's father relies entirely on secondary genealogical databases without primary source citations. The "Sir" title is almost certainly a data entry error or conflation. The Lancashire origin claim is plausible but unverified. The destruction of most early Westmoreland County records makes primary source confirmation unlikely without physical archive research.

## 3. John Faulkner Jr. (1678, 1727), Kent/Queen Anne's County, Maryland

Son of John Thomas Faulkner Sr. Vault files include: [[Faulkner]], [[Falconar_or_Forkner_or_Faulkner_or_Falkner]], and related spelling variant files.

### Records Found

**Will of John Faulkner, proven 9 May 1727, Queen Anne's County, Maryland.** John Faulkner wrote his will on 20 March 1727 and died the following month. The will was proven at Queen Anne's County. He named his wife Sarah (nee Ford) as sole Executrix and made her responsible for their underage children: Thomas, James, William, Emanuel, and daughter Ann. The will appears in Volume 6 of the Maryland Calendar of Wills. Source: [WikiTree, John Faulkner (1678, 1727)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1064); [Geni, John Faulkner](https://www.geni.com/people/John-Faulkner/6000000010412913316).

**Inventory of John Falconar, Queen Anne's County, 1727.** An inventory of his estate was filed. The record is cited as: "Inventory of John Falconar late of Queen Anns Co. 1727. Queen Anns Co., Md Adm. Papers File No. 191." Source: [WikiTree, John Faulkner (1678, 1727)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1064).

**Marriage record reference.** John Faulkner married Sarah Elizabeth Ford c.1703 in Anne Arundel County, Maryland. Source: [WikiTree, Sarah Elizabeth (Ford) Faulkner](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Ford-3321).

**Location and county formation.** John was born in 1678 at the family plantation on Hog Pen Neck, then in Kent County, Maryland. The land was reassigned to the newly formed Queen Anne's County in 1706. John was described as a planter and land owner. Source: [WikiTree, John Faulkner (1678, 1727)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1064).

**Children documented.** Six sons and two daughters between 1704 and 1720: John III, Thomas, James, William, Emanuel Ford Faulkner (documented extensively in pass 1), Ann, and others. Source: [WikiTree, John Faulkner (1678, 1727)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1064).

### Records Not Found

- No specific land patent or deed for John Faulkner Jr. was located by name. He is described as a "planter and land owner" but the acreage and patent details were not found.
- No Queen Anne's County court records naming John Faulkner were located in digitized collections.
- The actual text of the will was not found; only the abstract reference to Maryland Calendar of Wills, Volume 6.
- No tax lists or tithe records from Kent or Queen Anne's County naming John Faulkner were located.

### Confidence

**Strong Signal.** The will and estate inventory are documented with specific archival references (Maryland Calendar of Wills Vol. 6; Queen Anne's County Administration Papers File No. 191). The surname spelling variants (Faulkner, Falconar, Falkner, Forkner) are well documented and do not indicate separate individuals. The Hog Pen Neck plantation location and Queen Anne's County formation date are verifiable facts.

## 4. Burwell Family, Colonial Virginia

Multiple vault files exist: [[Burwell]], [[Burwell_Esq.]], [[Burwell_III]], [[Burwell_II_Major_._7MGF]], [[Burwell_Jr]], [[Burwell_son_of_Edmund_BurwellMargery_Alverd]], [[Burwell_son_of_Edward_Houghton_Burwell_II_1husb_of_Lucy_Higginson]]. Focus on Edmund Burwell and the broader Burwell lineage in colonial Virginia.

### Records Found

**Lewis Burwell (bapt. 1621/22, d. c.1653), founding patriarch.** Baptized 5 March 1621/22 at Ampthill, Bedfordshire, England. Acquired his first land in Virginia in 1648: 2,300 acres on the south bank of the lower York River (joint patent with a friend), followed by 2,350 acres on the north bank of the York River near Rosewell Creek and Bacon Point, claimed through headrights inherited from his stepfather. At death he owned approximately 7,000 acres. Married Lucy Higginson in 1650. Source: [Encyclopedia Virginia, Lewis Burwell](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/burwell-lewis-bap-1622-ca-1652/); [Wikipedia, Burwell family of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwell_family_of_Virginia).

**Fairfield Plantation, Gloucester County.** The Burwell family seat was Fairfield Plantation in Gloucester County, Virginia, established after 1648. Multiple generations of Burwells lived at Carter's Creek in Gloucester County. Source: [Wikipedia, Burwell family of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwell_family_of_Virginia).

**Governor Spotswood's 1713 complaint.** Governor Alexander Spotswood wrote in 1713 that "the greater part of the present Council are related to the Family of Burwells," indicating the family's extensive intermarriage with Virginia's colonial political elite. Source: [Wikipedia, Burwell family of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwell_family_of_Virginia).

**Burwell Family Papers, 1736, 1810.** The Virginia Museum of History and Culture holds Burwell Family Papers (MS 64.4) dating from 1736 to 1786, with additional materials through 1810. This collection would potentially contain correspondence, land documents, and legal records for the family. Source: [University of Virginia, Burwell Family Papers finding aid](https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=cw/viwc00105.xml); [Virginia Museum of History and Culture, Burwell Family Section 6](https://virginiahistory.org/research/research-resources/indexes/burwell-family-section-6).

**First Families of Virginia status.** The Burwells are recognized among the First Families of Virginia. Source: [Wikipedia, Burwell family of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwell_family_of_Virginia).

### Records Not Found

- No will, land patent, or court record specifically naming "Edmund Burwell" was located in online search results. The vault file [[Burwell_son_of_Edmund_BurwellMargery_Alverd]] suggests Edmund Burwell married Margery Alverd, but no primary source for Edmund Burwell's individual records appeared in any search.
- No entries from "Cavaliers and Pioneers" (the standard abstract of Virginia land patents) naming Edmund Burwell specifically were found online, though the Lewis Burwell 1648 patents are documented.
- The Burwell Family Papers (1736, 1810) at the Virginia Museum of History and Culture were identified but not accessible online; physical archive research would be required.
- No Gloucester County or York County court order book entries naming Edmund Burwell were located.

### Confidence

**Moderate Signal.** The Burwell family is extensively documented in secondary literature and archival finding aids, but the specific Edmund Burwell connection remains undocumented in online primary sources. The Lewis Burwell land patents (1648) are well attested. The Burwell Family Papers at the Virginia Museum of History and Culture represent a promising but unexamined archival collection. The vault's multiple Burwell files suggest a complex multi-generational lineage that would benefit from a dedicated deep dive into the "Cavaliers and Pioneers" abstracts and Gloucester County records.

## 5. Samuel Argall (Knight, Deputy Governor of Virginia)

Vault files: [[Argall_Knight_-Deputy_Govenor_of_Virginia]], [[ArgallKNIGHT_-Deputy_Govenor_of_Virginia]], and multiple related Argall files.

### Records Found

**Biographical dates.** Baptized c.1580 (sources vary between 1572 and 1580), died 1626. English sea captain, navigator, and Deputy Governor of Virginia. Source: [Encyclopedia Virginia, Samuel Argall](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/argall-samuel-bap-1580-1626/); [Wikipedia, Samuel Argall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Argall).

**Deputy Governorship, May 1617 to April 1619.** Argall arrived in Virginia about 15 May 1617 and assumed the office of deputy governor to the absent Lord De La Warr. During his tenure he prioritized economic measures to render the colony self sustaining, emphasizing commodity production and private investment. Source: [Encyclopedia Virginia, Samuel Argall](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/argall-samuel-bap-1580-1626/); [Britannica, Samuel Argall](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Samuel-Argall).

**Knighthood, 26 June 1622.** Argall was knighted by King James I on 26 June 1622 in recognition of his naval and colonial services. Source: [Wikipedia, Samuel Argall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Argall); [Find a Grave, Sir Samuel Argall](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/59758002/samuel-argall).

**Argall's Gift plantation.** A private tract approximately one mile north of Jamestown, granted as part of Argall's land policy to reward service with land allocations. Argall held a substantial patent of several thousand acres. Source: [Wikipedia, Samuel Argall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Argall); [American Heritage Partners, Remembering Samuel Argall](https://americanheritagepartners.net/2026/01/24/remembering-samuel-argall/).

**Land policy administration.** Argall advanced land policies granting sub patents and private plantations to company loyalists and investors, encouraging the establishment of outlying settlements beyond Jamestown. He also forced free ancient planters to clear land from a parcel granted to him by the London Company, and used the colony's corn stock to farm it. This arbitrary conduct contributed to complaints against his governance. Source: [Encyclopedia Virginia, Samuel Argall](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/argall-samuel-bap-1580-1626/); [Project Gutenberg, Mother Earth: Land Grants in Virginia 1607, 1699](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/28499/28499-h/28499-h.htm).

**Navigation achievement, 1609.** Argall was the first to determine a shorter northern route from England across the Atlantic to Virginia. Source: [Wikipedia, Samuel Argall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Argall).

### Records Not Found

- No specific text of a land patent issued to Samuel Argall personally was located, though secondary sources confirm he held "several thousand acres."
- No will or probate record for Samuel Argall was found. He died in 1626, and records from this period are extremely scarce.
- No Virginia Company of London minute book entries specifically about Argall's land grants were located in digitized form, though these records exist at the Library of Congress and the National Archives (UK).
- No Jamestown court records from Argall's tenure as deputy governor were found online.

### Confidence

**Strong Signal** for biographical facts (knighthood date, deputy governorship dates, Argall's Gift). **Moderate Signal** for specific land patent details, which are described in secondary sources but not individually cited from primary documents. Argall is a well documented historical figure in colonial Virginia historiography, and additional records likely exist in the Virginia Company archives and the Colonial Papers collection at the Library of Virginia (finding aid available at [Library of Virginia, Colonial Papers 1630, 1778](https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi03126.xml)).
