# Monroe Presidential Connection: Analysis and Evidence

## Claimed Line of Descent

The family tradition asserts the following chain connecting to President James Monroe:

1. **Colonel Spence Monroe** (1727, Westmoreland Co., VA; d. 1774, Westmoreland Co., VA) married **Elizabeth Jones** (c. 1730; d. c. 1772/1774)
2. Their alleged daughter **Rosamond Monroe** (b. 1750) married a Mr. Jones, becoming **Rosamond Jones**
3. Rosamond Jones (1750; d. c. 1821) married **William F Bourne Sr** (1743; d. 1836) of Grayson County, VA
4. Their daughter **Elizabeth Bourne** (b. March 20, 1785; d. August 23, 1866) married **Captain Lewis Hale Jr** (b. May 14, 1781; d. May 24, 1842)
5. Under this claim, President **James Monroe** (1758; d. 1831) was a sibling of Rosamond, making him a great uncle in the line

## Section 1: Colonel Spence Monroe (1727, 1774)

### Documented Facts

Spence Monroe (also recorded as Andrew Spence Monroe) was a moderately prosperous planter and carpenter in Westmoreland County, Virginia. He was the son of **Andrew Monroe** and **Christian Tyler**. He married **Elizabeth Jones** in 1752, daughter of **James Jones** and **Hester Davis**. The couple farmed approximately 500 acres on Monroe Creek.

Spence Monroe built the family home circa 1752, a structure of less than 1,600 square feet. He joined the Northern Neck Farmers in 1766 to protest the Stamp Act.

He died on February 16, 1774, at approximately age 47 in Monroe Hall, Westmoreland County, Virginia.

### Will and Probate

Spence Monroe's probate records are held in the Westmoreland County Wills collection (1654, 1800), available through Ancestry.com and the Library of Virginia. The specific contents of his will were not retrieved in this research pass. **This is a critical gap**: if Spence Monroe's will names a daughter Rosamond, that would constitute strong documentary evidence. If it does not, or if it names only the five documented children, the claimed connection is significantly weakened.

### Critical Finding: Rosamond Is Not Listed

No mainstream historical biography of James Monroe, no presidential reference, and no peer reviewed genealogy lists a sibling named "Rosamond" among Spence Monroe's children. The James Monroe Museum at the University of Mary Washington, the Miller Center at the University of Virginia, the White House Historical Association, and the American Battlefield Trust all identify only five children.

Some user contributed genealogy databases (Geni.com) include additional names such as Margaret E. Monroe, Sarah Monroe, and Isaac Monroe among Spence's children, but even these expanded lists do not consistently include a Rosamond.

## Section 3: Rosamond Jones Bourne (1750, c. 1821)

### Documented Facts

Rosamond "Rosa" Jones was born on February 14, 1750, and died circa 1821. She married **William Bourne** (also spelled Bourn) circa 1765 in Hanover County, Virginia, near Richmond. He was 22 and she was 15 at the time of marriage.

The couple settled in what became Grayson County, Virginia (created from Wythe County in 1792). William Bourne was elected Clerk of the Court, a position he held for 17 years.

### Children of William and Rosamond Bourne

According to the Bourne family records (McCarter family genealogy site):

1. **Patience** (November 18, 1770), married Jonathan Thomas
2. **Milly** (March 7, 1773), married Jessie McKinney
3. **Charity** (November 7, 1775), married John Blair
4. **Stephen** (February 26, 1779), married Patsy Mays
5. **Mary** (January 5, 1782), married Martin Dickinson
6. **Elizabeth** (March 20, 1785), married Captain Lewis Hale
7. **Frances** (June 5, 1788), married Stephen Hale Sr
8. **Celia** (December 25, 1790), married Robert Johnstone, Roaring River, Wilkes County, NC
9. **William Jr** (May 4, 1794), married Mary Johnstone, Roaring River, Wilkes County, NC

### Rosamond's Parentage: Conflicting Accounts

This is the central problem for the presidential connection. Three different accounts of Rosamond's parents exist:

**Account 1: Thomas and Bette Jones.** The Bourne family genealogy (McCarter family site) states: "Rosa was born in 1750 and grew up on a large land grant near Fredericksburg, Virginia. Her parents were Thomas and Bette Jones." This source also identifies her siblings as Naomi and three brothers: Minetree Jones Sr, Thomas, and Daniel.

**Account 2: Minitree Jones and Mary Elizabeth Spottswood.** Another family genealogy, maintained for approximately 200 years, records Rosamond Jones (1750, 1821) as the first born child of Minitree Thomas Jones (1723, 1808) and Mary Elizabeth Spottswood (1732, 1791). WikiTree records this parentage at [Jones-12381](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-12381). Minitree Jones has his own WikiTree profile at [Jones-12412](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-12412).

**Account 3: Spence Monroe (the claim under investigation).** The family tradition states Rosamond was born a Monroe, daughter of Colonel Spence Monroe, and married a Mr. Jones before (or instead of) marrying William Bourne.

### Analysis of the Three Accounts

Account 1 and Account 2 may describe the same family from different angles: "Thomas" could be a reference to "Minitree Thomas Jones," and "Bette" could be a nickname for Mary Elizabeth Spottswood. The blog "The Ancestral Clark" (vtcrewcat.wordpress.com) discusses the confusion around these names in detail.

Account 3 (the Monroe connection) is not supported by any of the primary Bourne or Jones family records found in this research. The documented Jones parentage for Rosamond does not intersect with the Monroe family. Spence Monroe's wife was Elizabeth Jones (daughter of James Jones and Hester Davis), while Rosamond's father was from the Thomas/Minitree Jones line. These are apparently different branches of the Jones surname in Virginia.

## Section 4: Elizabeth Jones Monroe (Wife of Spence Monroe)

Elizabeth Jones (c. 1730; d. c. 1772 or 1774) was the wife of Spence Monroe and the mother of President James Monroe. She was the daughter of **James Jones** and **Hester Davis**. James Monroe himself described her in 1830 as "a very amiable and respectable woman, possessing the best domestic qualities, a good wife and good parent."

Elizabeth died circa 1772, reportedly after giving birth to her youngest child. Some sources place her death in 1774, the same year as Spence Monroe's death.

Her brother, **Joseph Jones**, was a prominent Virginia politician (delegate to the Continental Congress, judge) who became guardian of the Monroe children after both parents died.

## Section 5: William F Bourne Sr (1743, 1836)

William Bourne was born in 1743, the son of **Stephen and Hannah Bourne** of Louisa County, Virginia. He married Rosamond Jones circa 1765. He died in 1836 at approximately 93 years of age.

William was among those who lobbied the Virginia State Legislature to create Grayson County from Wythe County in 1792. The county was named for William Grayson, one of Virginia's first U.S. senators. William Bourne was elected Clerk of the Court, serving for 17 years.

## Section 6: Elizabeth Bourne and Captain Lewis Hale Jr

Elizabeth Bourne (b. March 20, 1785, Grayson County, VA; d. August 23, 1866, Elk Creek, Grayson County, VA) married Captain Lewis Hale Jr (b. May 14, 1781, Elk Creek, Grayson County, VA; d. May 24, 1842, Elk Creek, Grayson County, VA).

Lewis Hale Jr was the son of **Captain Lewis Hale Sr** (c. 1742/1746; d. 1802), who was one of the first Justices of Grayson County in 1792, a Revolutionary War soldier (1781), and builder of Hale's Meeting House on Elk Creek, reputedly the earliest church in the county. Lewis Hale Sr married **Celia White**, daughter of Benjamin and Susan Bartee White.

Lewis Hale Jr followed his father in civil service in Grayson County. He and Elizabeth Bourne had **14 children**. His sister Frances Bourne (Elizabeth's sister) married Stephen Hale Sr (Lewis's brother), further binding the two families.

### Thomas L Hail

The user mentions "Thomas L Hail" as Elizabeth Bourne's husband. Research found a **Thomas Hail Sr (1776, 1835)** on Geni.com, but this individual does not appear to be the same person as Lewis Hale Jr/Captain Lewis Hale. The Hale/Hail/Haile surname was spelled variably in Grayson County records, but the documented husband of Elizabeth Bourne is consistently identified as **Lewis Hale** (sometimes spelled Haile). "Thomas L Hail" may be a different family member, a confusion of names, or a second marriage. No source found in this research identifies Thomas L Hail as Elizabeth Bourne's husband; all sources identify Lewis Hale.

## Section 7: The Bourne, Monroe, Jones Connection: How the Claim May Have Arisen

The claimed connection rests on identifying Rosamond Jones (wife of William Bourne) as a daughter of Spence Monroe who married into the Jones family. However, there is a much simpler explanation for the "Monroe" association:

**Spence Monroe's wife was Elizabeth Jones.** Rosamond was also a Jones. Both families lived in the general Fredericksburg/Tidewater Virginia area. It is plausible that a later family historian noticed the Jones surname connection and hypothesized (or received oral tradition suggesting) that Rosamond Jones was related to the Monroes through the Jones line. Over time, the connection may have been inflated from "related to the Monroes through the Jones family" to "Rosamond was a Monroe by birth."

The documentary evidence, as it stands, does not support the claim that Rosamond Jones was born Rosamond Monroe. Her documented parents were from the Jones family (Thomas/Minitree Jones line), not the Monroe family.

## Documentary Strength Assessment

### What Is Well Documented

- **Strong Signal**: Spence Monroe (1727, 1774) was the father of President James Monroe. This is established historical fact.
- **Strong Signal**: William Bourne (1743, 1836) married Rosamond Jones (1750, c. 1821) and they had nine children, including Elizabeth Bourne.
- **Strong Signal**: Elizabeth Bourne (1785, 1866) married Captain Lewis Hale Jr (1781, 1842) of Grayson County, VA.
- **Strong Signal**: Spence Monroe had five documented children: Elizabeth, James, Spence Jr, Andrew, and Joseph Jones Monroe.

### What Is Not Supported

- **Speculative**: The claim that Rosamond Jones was born Rosamond Monroe, daughter of Colonel Spence Monroe. No primary source, will, church record, or contemporary document has been found linking Rosamond to the Monroe family. All documented sources for Rosamond's parentage point to the Jones family (Thomas Jones or Minitree Jones).
- **Speculative**: The claim that Rosamond Monroe married a Mr. Jones before marrying William Bourne. There is no documented first marriage to a Jones. All sources indicate Rosamond was born a Jones and married William Bourne.

### What Would Strengthen or Refute the Claim

1. **Spence Monroe's will (1774)**: If his will names a daughter Rosamond, the connection moves from Speculative to Moderate Signal. The will is in the Westmoreland County records and should be obtainable from the Library of Virginia or through Ancestry.com's Virginia Wills and Probate Records (1652, 1900) collection.
2. **Westmoreland County church records (1740s, 1770s)**: Baptismal records for the Monroe family could confirm or deny additional children.
3. **Elizabeth Jones Monroe's family records**: More detail on the James Jones / Hester Davis family could clarify whether there is any overlap with the Thomas/Minitree Jones line that produced Rosamond.
4. **Land records in Westmoreland County**: Transfers from Spence Monroe's estate to his children could confirm the number and names of heirs.
5. **Marriage records in Hanover County, VA, c. 1765**: The marriage record of William Bourne and Rosamond Jones could identify her father by name.

## Open Questions

1. Does Spence Monroe's will (1774, Westmoreland County) name any children beyond the five documented ones?
2. Is there a blood relationship between the James Jones / Hester Davis family (parents of Elizabeth Jones Monroe) and the Thomas/Minitree Jones family (parents of Rosamond Jones Bourne)? If so, the families are connected through the Jones line, not the Monroe line.
3. Who exactly was Thomas L Hail, and what is his relationship to the Bourne or Hale families? Is this a misidentification or confusion with Lewis Hale Jr?
4. Are there any Grayson County or Wythe County records (deeds, court minutes, tax lists) that describe Rosamond Bourne as "nee Monroe" or reference a Monroe maiden name?
5. When and how did the oral tradition of a Monroe connection originate in the family? Is there a specific family bible entry or letter that makes this claim?
6. Some genealogical databases list additional children for Spence Monroe (Margaret E., Sarah, Isaac). Are these documented in primary sources, and could Rosamond be among undocumented daughters?

## Negative Results Log

- Searched for "Rosamond Monroe" as daughter of Spence Monroe: no primary source documentation found. Only user contributed genealogy databases reference this connection.
- Searched for Spence Monroe's will contents: will exists in Westmoreland County records but specific text was not available online in this research pass.
- Searched for "Thomas L Hail" as husband of Elizabeth Bourne: no source found. All documented sources identify Lewis Hale as Elizabeth Bourne's husband.
- Searched for Rosamond's first marriage to a "Mr. Jones": no evidence found. All sources indicate she was born Jones, not that she married into the Jones family.
- No scholarly biography of James Monroe (Miller Center, White House Historical Association, American Battlefield Trust) lists a sibling named Rosamond.

## Summary

The documented chain from Elizabeth Bourne through Rosamond Jones Bourne to William Bourne, and Elizabeth Bourne's marriage to Captain Lewis Hale, is well supported by multiple independent sources. The critical link that is **not** supported is the claim that Rosamond Jones was born Rosamond Monroe, daughter of Colonel Spence Monroe. All primary and secondary sources for Rosamond's parentage point to the Jones family. No mainstream source on James Monroe's family lists a sibling named Rosamond. Until primary documents (particularly Spence Monroe's will or Westmoreland County church/land records) can be examined, this presidential connection should be classified as **Speculative**.
