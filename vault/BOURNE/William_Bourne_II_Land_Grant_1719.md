# Land Grant of William Bourne II (1719)

## Source

Virginia Land Patent Records. Lt. Governor Alexander Spotswood issued the patent on 11 July 1719. Referenced in Louisa County Land Book No. 10.

## Patent Details

**Date**: 11 July 1719
**Grantee**: William Bourn of New Kent County
**Acreage**: 300 acres
**Location**: Both sides of Neck Creek (later Duncan's Creek, now Jones Creek), south of the South Anna River, near the Hanover County line.
**Consideration**: "For and in consideration of the sum of five shillings" and "the Importation of five persons to dwell within this our said Colony."

## Jurisdictional History

The land was originally in New Kent County. It became part of Hanover County when that county was created in 1721, and then part of Louisa County when Louisa was created in 1742.

## Legacy

The land remained in Bourne family possession for almost one hundred years. Eighty acres are still carried on the books of Louisa County as "formerly William Bourne, Senior's land." The property was inherited by his son William Bourn III.

Note: The loss of Hanover County records for this period makes it impossible to determine precisely when William Bourne II died.

## Connection to Grayson County Bournes

William Bourne II's grandsons William, Stephen, and John grew up on this 300 acre land grant. They later became pioneer settlers of Grayson County, Virginia. Stephen Bourne (b. 1744) and William Bourne Sr. (b. 1743) moved to the Elk Creek area in what was then Montgomery County (later Wythe, then Grayson County formed 1792). The first Grayson County Court was held in the Bourne barn on 21 May 1793.

**Strong Signal.** Virginia land patents are official government records preserved at the Library of Virginia. The 1719 patent by Spotswood is well documented. Referenced in WikiTree (Bourne-559) and Louisa County Land Book No. 10.
