# Colonial Records: Jones, Minitree, Churchill, and Bourne Families

Research audit focused on colonial Virginia records for the ancestral chain: Rosamond Jones (1750, 1821) > Minitree Thomas Jones (1723, 1808) > Capt. Thomas Churchill Jones (1697, 1756) married Martha Elizabeth Minitree (1702, 1788).

---

## 1. Capt. Thomas Churchill Jones (1697, 1756)

**Confidence: Moderate Signal**

Note on naming: Colonial and genealogical sources consistently refer to this individual as "Churchill Jones" rather than "Thomas Churchill Jones." The name "Churchill" was his given name, inherited from his mother Mary Churchill. The addition of "Thomas" as a first name may be a later genealogical interpolation. All primary source references found use "Churchill Jones."

### Parents

Source: [WikiTree Jones-21008](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-21008); [WikiTree Churchill-315](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Churchill-315). Churchill Jones was the son of Roger Jones and Mary Churchill of Christ Church Parish, Middlesex County, Virginia. Mary Churchill was a daughter of Col. William Churchill (1649, 1710), the prominent Middlesex County burgess and Virginia Governor's Council member.

### Birth Record

Source: Christ Church Parish Register, Middlesex County, Virginia. Churchill Jones was born 1697/1698 in Middlesex County. The parish register lists him among seven children of Roger and Mary Jones: Susannah (b. 1695), Churchill (b. 1697/8), Judah (b. 1702), Thomas (b. 1704), Mary (b. 1706/7), Roger (b. 1709, d. 1720), and Catherine (b. 1716).

### Church and Civil Service

Source: Christ Church Parish vestry records, Middlesex County. Churchill Jones served as clerk of the vestry of Christ Church Parish continuously from 1722 to 1749. He was also described as a prominent landowner in Middlesex County.

### Mother's Will (1743)

Source: Will of Mary (Churchill) Jones, dated 24 July 1743, Middlesex County. Churchill Jones is named in his mother's will. Col. William Churchill's will (probated 10 March 1710/11) also identifies Mary Jones and two of her children, Churchill Jones and Susannah Jones.

### Marriage to Millicent Blackburn (1744)

Source: Middlesex County court records. Churchill Jones married Millicent Blackburn in 1744, daughter of Captain William Blackburn. A court order from 2 July 1745 involved dividing slaves per the will of Captain William Blackburn between Edward Blackburne and Millicent Jones, wife of Churchill Jones.

### Discrepancy: Marriage to Martha Elizabeth Minitree

**No primary source was found** confirming a marriage between Churchill Jones (1697, 1756) and Martha Elizabeth Minitree (1702, 1788). The documented wife is Millicent Blackburn (married 1744). Some genealogical databases also list a later marriage to Anne Kemp in 1756. The Martha Elizabeth Minitree marriage appears in family trees on Geni, Ancestry, and similar platforms but lacks cited primary documentation. This is a significant gap requiring further research.

### Death

Source: [WikiTree Jones-21008](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-21008). Churchill Jones died 24 May 1756 in Middlesex County, Colony of Virginia, at age 59. His will (1757) named Colonel Edmund Berkeley as one of the guardians for his children.

### Negative Results

Searched for Virginia colonial land patents specifically naming Churchill Jones: no patents found in online indexes. The Library of Virginia's land patent records (1623, 1774) and county deed indexes would need to be consulted directly. No military commission record was located online, despite the "Captain" title used in some family trees.

## 2. Martha Elizabeth Minitree (1702, 1788)

**Confidence: Speculative**

### The Minitree/Menestrier Immigrant Origin

Source: [WikiTree Minestrier-1](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Minestrier-1); [Olive Tree Genealogy ship list](https://www.olivetreegenealogy.com/ships/hugship02.shtml); [Huguenot Society of the Founders of Manakin](https://www.huguenotmanakin.org/manakintown-huguenot-ship-list). The Minitree surname derives from "Menestrier," a French Huguenot family. David Menestrier arrived on the ship "Ye Peter and Anthony" at James City, Virginia on 20 September 1700. He was a blacksmith who had fled France following the revocation of the Edict of Nantes (1685). He and his brother Paul traveled from Ardeche through Switzerland and Berlin before emigrating.

### David Menestrier in Colonial Records

Source: [Colonial Williamsburg research](https://nelsonloeb.wordpress.com/2017/02/13/laying-bricks-in-colonial-williamsburg/). The elder David Minitree (also named David) was a blacksmith whose name appears in colonial Williamsburg construction records. He worked on both the Capitol building and the Public Gaol, constructed after the capital moved from Jamestown to Williamsburg in 1699. He died circa 1712.

### The Spelling Variation

The surname appears in records as: Menestrier, Minestra, Minatra, Minatree, Minetree, Minutree, and Minitree. This variation is typical of French Huguenot names anglicized in colonial Virginia records.

### Jacob (Andrew) Minitree

Source: [Ancestry records](https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/elizabeth-minitree-24-10sgd8g). Jacob Andrew Minitree appears in records connected to King George County, Virginia. His daughter Elizabeth Minitree was born circa 1730 in Potomac, King George County, Virginia, to Jacob Andrew Minitree and Rosamond Holland. If Martha Elizabeth Minitree (b. 1702) was connected to this Jacob, the generation timing is plausible (Jacob being a son or nephew of immigrant David).

### Negative Results

No primary source was found documenting Martha Elizabeth Minitree's birth, death, or marriage to Churchill Jones. The name appears in compiled genealogies and online family trees but without parish register citations, court records, or other primary documentation. The connection between the Middlesex County Jones family and the Minitree family (who settled near Manakin Town and later in King George County/Petersburg) has not been substantiated with a specific record.

## 3. Minitree Thomas Jones (1723, 1808)

**Confidence: Moderate Signal**

### Birth and Parentage

Source: [WikiTree Jones-12412](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-12412); [The Ancestral Clark blog](https://vtcrewcat.wordpress.com/2018/05/04/understanding-minitree-jones/). Minitree Thomas Jones was born circa 1723 and died 21 March 1808 in Grayson County, Virginia, at age 84. He is listed as the son of Churchill Jones (1697, 1756) and Martha Elizabeth Minitree (1702, 1788), though as noted above, the Martha Minitree marriage lacks primary documentation.

### Marriage to Mary Elizabeth Spottswood

Source: [WikiTree Spottswood-7](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Spottswood-7). Minitree Jones married a woman identified in family tradition as "Miss Spottswood." Some genealogical sources name her Mary Elizabeth Spottswood (1732, 1791) and claim descent from Lt. Gov. Alexander Spotswood (1676, 1740). However, no primary source confirming this lineage has been identified. The WikiTree profile notes this connection remains unproven. The Ancestral Clark blog also flags this as uncertain.

### Children

Source: Multiple compiled genealogies. Minitree and Mary had children including:

- Rosamond Jones (b. 1750, d. circa 1821), married William Bourne
- Minitree Jones Jr. (b. 1752, d. 1821)
- Spottswood Jones (b. 1754, d. 1826)
- Churchill Jones (b. 1756, d. 1827)

All four children are described as having served during the Revolutionary War era.

### Grayson County Settlement

Source: [New River Notes, Pioneer Settlers of Grayson County](https://www.newrivernotes.com/pioneer-settlers-of-grayson-county-virginia/). Minitree Jones was one of the earliest founding fathers of Grayson County, Virginia. He does not appear in deed records until Grayson County was established in 1792. Prior to that date, the area was part of Montgomery County (formed 1776 from Fincastle County).

### Public Service

Source: Grayson County records (cited in compiled genealogies). In 1798, Minitree Jones served as a Justice of the Peace in Grayson County and was appointed Major of the First Battalion.

### Land Transactions

Source: Grayson County deed records. William Jones, with George and Thomas Jones of Grayson County, and James Jones of Henrico County, sold land in 1808 in Grayson County to Isaac Garretson (Garrison). This transaction occurred in the year of Minitree Jones's death.

### Negative Results

No will or probate record for Minitree Thomas Jones was located in online indexes. Hanover County records were searched but no deeds or tax records naming Minitree Jones were found online. The Library of Virginia's Wills and Estate Records Index and the Hanover County Clerk's office would need to be consulted directly. The period between his birth (circa 1723 in Middlesex County) and his appearance in Grayson County records (1792) represents a gap of nearly 70 years with no documented records found.

## 4. The Churchill Family Connection

**Confidence: Strong Signal (for the Churchill line); Moderate Signal (for the Jones connection)**

### Col. William Churchill (1649, 1710)

Source: [Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/churchhill-william-1649-1710/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Churchill_(burgess)). William Churchill emigrated from England to Virginia, becoming a merchant, planter, and politician in Middlesex County. He served in the House of Burgesses and on the Virginia Governor's Council (1705, 1710). His will was probated 10 March 1710/11 and names his daughter Mary Jones and her children Churchill Jones and Susannah Jones.

### The Jones/Churchill Intermarriage Pattern

Source: [Colonial Settlers database](https://www.colonial-settlers-md-va.us/getperson.php?personID=I051260&tree=tree1). The Churchill and Jones families intermarried across multiple generations in Middlesex County:

1. Mary Churchill (daughter of Col. William Churchill) married Roger Jones, producing Churchill Jones (b. 1697/8).
2. In the next generation, Churchill Jones (b. 1748, son of the elder Churchill Jones) married Judith Churchill (daughter of Armistead Churchill and Hannah Harrison) on 11 November 1769.
3. William Jones (b. 1750) married Elizabeth "Betty" Churchill, sister of Judith, also a daughter of Col. Armistead Churchill.

### Armistead Churchill as Guardian

Source: Churchill Jones's father's 1757 will. Col. Edmund Berkeley and members of the Churchill family were named as guardians for the young Churchill and William Jones after their father's death. William Churchill (son of Col. Armistead Churchill) served as executor and guardian.

### Naming Pattern Evidence

The recurrence of "Churchill" as a given name in the Jones family across generations (Churchill Jones b. 1697; Churchill Jones b. 1748; Churchill Jones b. 1756 in Grayson County) strongly supports the maternal Churchill lineage through Mary Churchill.

## 5. The Bourne Family and Jones Connection

**Confidence: Moderate Signal**

### William Bourne II Land Grant (1719)

Source: [McCarter Family research](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm); [RootsMagic site](https://sites.rootsmagic.com/mccarterfamily/individual.php?p=270). On 11 July 1719, William Bourne II received a land grant of 300 acres in New Kent County, Virginia. The grant was signed by Lt. Gov. Alexander Spotswood. The land was located south of the South Anna River, near the Hanover county line, on both sides of Narrow Neck Creek (later called Duncan's Creek, now Jones Creek). This area was in the part of New Kent County that became Hanover County in 1721 and then Louisa County in 1742.

### Rosamond Jones Married William Bourne

Source: [WikiTree Jones-12381](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-12381); [Genealogy.com forum](https://www.genealogy.com/forum/surnames/topics/jones/6571/). Rosamond Jones (b. 14 February 1750) married William Bourne (b. 1743) circa 1765 in Hanover County, Virginia. Rosamond was the eldest child of Minitree Thomas Jones and Mary Elizabeth Spottswood.

### Migration to Grayson County

Source: [McCarter Family research](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm). Soon after their marriage, William and Rosamond ("Rosa") Bourne left the colonial settlement and moved to the New River frontier, becoming early settlers of what would become Grayson County. Rosa Bourne is described as the "first missionary woman of Grayson County Virginia."

### Bourne Family on the 1719 Grant Land

William Bourne (husband of Rosamond) and his brothers Stephen and John grew up on the 300 acre land grant obtained by their grandfather William Bourne II. This land remained in the possession of Bourne descendants for nearly 100 years, and 80 acres were still carried on Louisa County books as "formerly William Bourne, Senior's land."

### Children of William and Rosamond Bourne

Source: Compiled genealogies. Their children included: Patience (1770), Stephen (1779), Milly (1773), Charity (1775), Mary (1782), Elizabeth (1785), Frances (1788), Celia (1790), William (1794), and sons named Spottswood and Churchill (reflecting the Jones family naming tradition).

### Notable Detail: "Jones Creek"

The renaming of Narrow Neck Creek (on the Bourne land grant) to "Jones Creek" may indicate a Jones family presence on or near this land, though no specific Jones deed for that tract was found.

## 6. The Spottswood Connection

**Confidence: Speculative**

### The Claim

Multiple compiled genealogies state that Minitree Thomas Jones married Mary Elizabeth Spottswood, claimed as a descendant of Lt. Gov. Alexander Spotswood (1676, 1740).

### The Evidence Gap

Source: [WikiTree Spottswood-7](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Spottswood-7). The WikiTree profile for Mary Elizabeth Spottswood notes that the connection to Governor Spotswood is unproven. No primary source (parish register, will, deed) has been found naming her parents. The Ancestral Clark blog, which conducted detailed research on Minitree Jones, also flagged this as uncertain. Governor Spotswood's known children (John Spotswood and Anne Catherine Spotswood) are well documented, and no "Mary Elizabeth" appears among them.

### What Is Known

The Spottswood surname does appear in the Jones family naming pattern: Minitree Jones named a son "Spottswood Jones," and the Bourne grandchildren also carried the name. The 1719 Bourne land grant was signed by Alexander Spotswood as Lt. Governor, which places these families in overlapping geography and time, but does not establish a family relationship.

### Critical Open Question

The marriage of Churchill Jones to Martha Elizabeth Minitree is the foundational claim linking the Jones and Minitree families. No primary source has been located for this marriage. The documented wife of Churchill Jones (1697, 1756) is Millicent Blackburn (married 1744). Further research should focus on Middlesex County marriage bonds, Christ Church Parish registers, and any Minitree records in King George County to resolve this discrepancy.

### Recommended Next Steps

1. Request Middlesex County marriage bonds and Christ Church Parish register searches from the Library of Virginia for any Jones/Minitree marriage.
2. Search King George County deed and court records for Jacob Minitree and any connection to the Middlesex County Jones family.
3. Search Grayson County Will Book for Minitree Jones's estate (died March 1808).
4. Search Hanover County deed records for Minitree Jones between 1740 and 1780 to fill the 70 year gap.
5. Investigate the "Captain" title attributed to Churchill Jones through colonial militia records at the Library of Virginia.
6. Search for Alexander Spotswood's estate records and any mention of a granddaughter Mary Elizabeth.
