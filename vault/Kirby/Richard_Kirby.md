## Biography

Richard Kirby (b. ca. 1614, Rowington, Warwickshire, England; d. 21 July 1688, Dartmouth, Bristol County, Massachusetts). In 1637, he joined William Wood and others from Lynn who settled Sandwich, Massachusetts, becoming one of the first eleven male members of the church there. In 1643, he appeared on the list of sixty eight Sandwich inhabitants able to bear arms. In 1651, he was presented for non attendance on public worship, coinciding with church disputes. In 1657, a complaint was filed that he, Nicolas Upsiall, and others met at William Allen's house on the Lord's Day. The Kirbys were fined as Quaker sympathizers (57 pounds, 12 shillings), though Richard may never have formally joined the Society of Friends; he took the "oath of fidelity" in 1684, which Quakers typically refused. By 1662, the Kirbys left Sandwich due to persecution. He purchased land in Dartmouth in November 1670, and additional land in 1683 from Zechariah Jenkins on the east side of the Cocksett River at Nasquamskiesett. (Sources: WikiTree Kirby-103; Caskey Family genealogy; Plymouth Colony Records)
