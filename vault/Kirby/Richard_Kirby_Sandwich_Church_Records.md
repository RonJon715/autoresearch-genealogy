# Richard Kirby: Sandwich, Massachusetts Church and Court Records

## Source

Sandwich Congregational Church records; Plymouth Colony Court records. Compiled from secondary genealogical sources.

## Church Membership (1637)

Richard Kirby was one of the first eleven male members of the Puritan Church of Sandwich, Massachusetts. He joined William Wood and others from Lynn who moved to Cape Cod in 1637 to settle Sandwich.

## Court Records and Civic Life

**1638**: Mentioned as a neighbor by Thomas Hampton of Sandwich in his will. Fined, with others, for having swine unringed.
**August 1643**: Named in the list of sixty eight inhabitants of Sandwich able to bear arms.
**1651**: Presented, with others, for non attendance on public worship. This coincided with a dispute between Rev. Mr. Leveridge and his congregation.

## Quaker Sympathies

**February 1657**: Complaint entered that Nicolas Upsiall, Richard Kerbey, the wife of John Newland, and others "did frequently meet together at the house of William Allen of Sandwich on the Lord's Day and at other times."

Richard Kirby and his son openly showed disapproval of the Puritan Church. They were fined as Quaker sympathizers. The Sandwich Quakers paid 679 pounds in fines total; the Kirbys paid 57 pounds and 12 shillings.

However, Richard Kirby may never have formally joined the Society of Friends. In 1684, he took the "oath of fidelity" (which Quakers typically refused on religious grounds). Neither his name nor his son Richard's appears in the membership records of the Friends.

## Removal to Dartmouth

By 1662, the Kirbys left Sandwich due to religious persecution. After 1660, Richard moved to Dartmouth, MA. In November 1670, he purchased land in Dartmouth. In 1683, he purchased land from Zechariah Jenkins on the east side of the Cocksett River at Nasquamskiesett.

## Name Spelling Variants

Records spell the surname as: Kirby, Kerbey, Kerby, Kerbe.

## Confidence

**Strong Signal.** Church and court records are primary sources, though accessed here through secondary compilations.
