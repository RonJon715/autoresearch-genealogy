# Colonial Records Search, Pass 3

Third pass search for colonial records across five families represented in the vault. Each section documents records found with citations, records not found with searches attempted, and a confidence assessment.

## 1. Filmer Family (Kent, England to Colonial Virginia/Maryland)

### Records Found

**Sir Robert Filmer, Kt. (c. 1588, d. 26 May 1653):**
The vault contains Sir Robert I Filmer (Fylmer), 1st Baron Filmer of Sutton (b. Jan 1525, d. 31 Aug 1585), at East Sutton Manor, Kent. His descendant Sir Robert Filmer (c. 1588, d. 1653) was the famed political philosopher and author of _Patriarcha_ (published posthumously in 1680). He was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, and Lincoln's Inn, and was knighted by Charles I. His house at East Sutton was sacked during the English Civil Wars, and he was imprisoned as a royalist. His son, also Robert, was created the first Filmer baronet in 1674.
Sources: [Britannica: Sir Robert Filmer](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Robert-Filmer); [Wikipedia: Robert Filmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Filmer); [Kent Archaeological Society](https://www.kentarchaeology.org.uk/journal/91/sir-robert-filmer-east-sutton).

**Filmer Baronetcy:**
The Filmer baronetcy of East Sutton Place, Kent, was created in 1674. The vault's Sir Edward Henry Filmer VII, Knight of Chester and High Sheriff of Kent (14 times), aligns with the documented line of Filmer baronets holding offices in Kent.
Source: [Wikipedia: Robert Filmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Filmer).

**Reginald Filmer (vault: [[Reginald_FilmerFylmerFilmar]]):**
Father of Elizabeth Filmer (Faulkner). The vault records him as a member of the East Sutton branch. No independent colonial land patent or will was found for Reginald Filmer in Virginia or Maryland databases.

### Records Not Found

Searched "Filmer colonial Virginia Maryland land patents wills baronetcy Kent England" across general web sources, Genealogy Trails, Rootsweb, FamilySearch, and Maryland Courts genealogy guides. No specific Filmer land patents in Virginia or Maryland were returned. The Filmer family appears to have been primarily Kent gentry; their colonial connection runs through the Faulkner marriage rather than direct Filmer land grants.

### Confidence: Moderate Signal

The baronetcy and Kent gentry status are well documented through published sources. The colonial connection appears indirect, via the Faulkner/Filmer marriage and emigration in 1665, rather than through Filmer family land patents in Virginia or Maryland.

## 2. Warner/Lewis Family (Colonial Virginia; Washington Connection)

### Records Found

**Augustine Warner Sr. (1611, d. 24 Dec 1674):**
Arrived in Virginia in 1628 aboard the _Hopewell_ as part of a group of 34 settlers brought by Adam Thoroughgood. In 1635, Warner patented 250 acres in New Poquoson, Elizabeth City County. Warner Hall Plantation originated from a 600 acre headright land grant awarded on 18 January 1642/3 for transporting twelve settlers. Beginning in 1654, he used headright patents to acquire land in Gloucester County across the York River. He served in both houses of the Virginia General Assembly.
Sources: [Wikipedia: Augustine Warner Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_Warner_Sr.); [WikiTree: Warner-97](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Warner-97).

**Augustine Warner Jr. (b. 3 Jun 1642, d. 19 Jun 1681):**
Served as burgess for Gloucester County from 1666 to 1677. Speaker of the House of Burgesses in 1676 and 1677 (before and after Bacon's Rebellion). Inherited Warner Hall in 1674. His estate was seized by Bacon's forces after the burning of Jamestown on 19 September 1676. He sued William Byrd I for damages. He then served on the Virginia Governor's Council from October 1677 until his death.
Sources: [Wikipedia: Augustine Warner Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_Warner_Jr.); [Virginia House of Delegates History](https://history.house.virginia.gov/members/2387).

**Washington Connection:**
Augustine Warner Sr. and Mary Townley Warner are documented as direct ancestors of George Washington, Queen Elizabeth II, Meriwether Lewis, Gen. Robert E. Lee, and Gen. George S. Patton. Augustine Warner Jr. is identified as the last common ancestor of George Washington and King Charles III. The vault file for [[Elizabeth_Isabelle_Warner_Lewis_George_Washingtons_aunt_William_the_Conqueror_Magna_Carta_Lineage]] (b. 24 Nov 1672, Chesapeake City, Virginia) aligns with this lineage.
Sources: [Wikipedia: Augustine Warner Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_Warner_Sr.); [Grokipedia: Augustine Warner Jr.](https://grokipedia.com/page/Augustine_Warner_Jr.).

### Records Not Found

The vault file for Elizabeth Isabelle Warner Lewis lists "Father: Unknown" and "Mother: Unknown." The web searches confirm the Warner family lineage but do not resolve her specific parentage. Further research in Gloucester County records or the _William and Mary Quarterly_ Reade Family article may help.

### Confidence: Strong Signal

The Warner family colonial records are extensively documented in published sources, legislative records, and land patent abstracts. The Washington connection is well established.

## 3. Lightfoot Family (Colonial Virginia Gentry)

### Records Found

**Philip Lightfoot I (c. 1646, d. Dec 1708):**
The vault's [[William_LightfootLightfoote]] (b. 1645, Tinshill, Adel, Yorkshire; d. Dec 1708) appears to correspond to Philip Lightfoot I in published colonial records. Philip Lightfoot I's will (dated 1708) left three tracts of land at "Sandy Point" (Wallingford Parish, James City County) to his son Francis.
Source: [WikiTree: Lightfoot-255](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Lightfoot-255).

**Philip Lightfoot II (c. 1689, d. 1748), "The Merchant Prince":**
Born in Gloucester County, Virginia. Appointed Clerk of York County in 1707; moved to Yorktown where he built the Lightfoot Mansion on a bluff commanding the port. He became extremely wealthy through tobacco exports and was known as "The Merchant Prince." He was sworn in as a member of the Council of Colonial Virginia in October 1733. He died in 1748, leaving a large estate. He married Mary Armistead.
Sources: [NPS: The Lightfoot Family in Yorktown](https://npshistory.com/publications/colo/lightfoot-family.pdf); [WikiTree: Lightfoot-245](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Lightfoot-245); [Wikipedia: Philip Lightfoot II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Lightfoot_II).

**William Lightfoot Papers (1740, 1764):**
William Lightfoot (1722, 1764) of Yorktown maintained mercantile account books (1747, 1764) with accounts for plantations and store trade across the colony and with British merchants.
Source: [Colonial Williamsburg Digital Library](https://research.colonialwilliamsburg.org/library/materials/manuscripts/view/index.cfm?id=LightfootW).

### Records Not Found

The vault's [[George_Lightfoot_IV]] (b. 6 Sep 1661, Cookridge, Yorkshire; d. c. 1709) is listed as son of William Lightfoot/Lightfoote and Lady Mary Bartlett. No colonial Virginia record was found linking this George Lightfoot IV to the Philip Lightfoot line. The vault records show Yorkshire origins (Cookridge, Tinshill, Adel), while the published Lightfoot colonial gentry records center on Gloucester County and Yorktown. It is unclear whether the vault's Lightfoot line is the same family as the documented colonial Lightfoot merchants, or a separate Yorkshire branch. This is an open question requiring further research.

### Confidence: Moderate Signal

The colonial Lightfoot family in Yorktown is well documented. The vault's Lightfoot line (Yorkshire origins) may or may not connect to the colonial Virginia Lightfoot gentry. The name overlap and approximate dates suggest a possible connection, but no confirming record was found.

## 4. Reade/Reede Family (Colonial Virginia Officials)

### Records Found

**Col. George Reade (b. 25 Oct 1608, d. 21 Nov 1671):**
Prominent landowner, military officer, and politician in colonial Virginia. He was appointed Secretary of State pro tem of the colony in 1640 (serving while Secretary Kemp traveled to England, 1640, 1642). He also served as clerk of the Virginia Governor's Council and as Acting Governor of Virginia during Governor Harvey's absence (1638, 1639), until Sir Francis Wyatt arrived.

**Land Patents:**
George Reade patented 600 acres in Lancaster County (1651), 500 acres in Northumberland County (1653), 2,000 acres in Westmoreland County (1657), and in 1667 the General Assembly granted him 2,000 acres along the Piankatank River in Gloucester County. He bequeathed 850 acres in York County to his firstborn sons George and Robert, subject to a life estate for his widow.

**Marriage and Yorktown Connection:**
Married Elizabeth Martiau, daughter of Nicolas Martiau ("father of Yorktown"). This connected Reade to land holdings in the Yorktown area inherited by Elizabeth from her father. He is buried at Grace Episcopal Churchyard, Yorktown.

**Washington Lineage:**
George Reade is the great great grandfather of George Washington, for whom Washington was reportedly named.
Sources: [Wikipedia: George Reade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Reade_(colonial_governor)); [Find a Grave: George Reade](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/5737079/george-read); [Captain James Davis Genealogy blog](https://captainjamesdavisgenealogy.wordpress.com/2013/02/08/colonel-george-reade-namesake-of-george-washington/); [JSTOR: Reade Family](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1916351).

### Records Not Found

The vault's [[James_Reade]] (b. bef. 15 Apr 1599, Brightling, Sussex) and [[Mary_Reade_Reede]] (b. c. 1606, Sussex) are listed with parents [[John_William_Faulkner]] and [[Martha_Faulkner_1574]]. This parentage links them to the Faulkner family rather than to Col. George Reade's line. No record was found confirming a connection between the vault's Reade/Reede individuals and Col. George Reade of Yorktown. The vault's Reade line may represent a separate Sussex family, or there may be a GEDCOM import error in the parentage fields.

### Confidence: Strong Signal (for Col. George Reade); Speculative (for vault's Reade/Reede connection to Faulkner family)

Col. George Reade's colonial records are extensive and well documented. The vault's Reade/Reede entries, with Faulkner parentage, do not match the documented Reade colonial line and require further verification.

## 5. Elizabeth Filmer (Wife of John Thomas Faulkner Sr.)

### Records Found

**Elizabeth Filmer (b. 1631, Kent, England; d. 1669):**
The vault file [[Elizabeth_Filmer_Faulkner]] records her as daughter of [[Reginald_FilmerFylmerFilmar]] and [[Jane_Travis]], born in Kent, England.

**Marriage and Emigration:**
Elizabeth married John Faulkner Sr. (c. 1628, d. after 1665). According to WikiTree and multiple genealogical databases, they emigrated from England, arriving on 22 January 1665 at Hog Pen Neck, Kent County, Maryland, aboard the ship _Agreement_ from Wales. They paid 300 pounds of tobacco for passage, brought two servants, and arrived with three infant sons: Francis, Thomas, and John Jr.

**Colonial Maryland Record:**
A February 1665 document records: "Know all men by these presents that I Edmond Barton of County of Kent, planter, do assign to Richard Fizallen these rights of these under written parties John Faulkner, Senr., John Faulkner, Junr. ... the seven first this year transported in Agreement. As witness by hand this Fourteenth day Feb. 1665 Edmond Barton." This confirms their arrival in Kent County, Maryland.

**Two Elizabeth Filmers:**
Research identified two women named Elizabeth Filmer in this family narrative: (1) John Faulkner's wife Elizabeth, daughter of Reginald Filmer; and (2) John Faulkner's mother, Lady Elizabeth Filmer, daughter of Sir Edward Filmer, who joined them in Kent County, Maryland in 1677. These two are cousins within the Filmer family.
Sources: [WikiTree: Filmer-66](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Filmer-66); [WikiTree: Filmer-52](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Filmer-52); [WikiTree: Faulkner-1146](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1146); [Geni: John Faulkner Sr.](https://www.geni.com/people/John-Faulkner-Sr/6000000010412760095).

### Records Not Found

Searched for Elizabeth Filmer's maiden family records in Kent, England (christening, parish records). The vault cites "England & Wales, Christening Index, 1530, 1980" as a source but no specific parish or date of christening was found in this search. The Maryland State Archives Early Settlers Index (1633, 1685) is available for further research on land patents associated with the Faulkner/Filmer family after arrival.

### Confidence: Moderate Signal

The emigration record and Kent County, Maryland arrival are confirmed through multiple sources. Elizabeth's Filmer parentage (daughter of Reginald Filmer) is attested in genealogical databases but not yet confirmed by primary parish records from Kent, England.
