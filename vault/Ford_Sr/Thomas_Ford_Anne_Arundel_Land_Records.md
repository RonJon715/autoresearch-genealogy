# Thomas Ford: Anne Arundel County, Maryland Land Records

## Source

Maryland Land Patent records; Anne Arundel County surveys. Compiled from WeRelate, Geni.com, and Skordas' *Early Settlers of Maryland*.

## Immigration

Thomas Ford entered Maryland circa 1650. A servant "Thos Ford" was transported to Maryland "before June 1652," listed among those William Ayres claimed for transporting to the colony on 5 October 1653. Given the proximity of Ford lands to Ayers/Chew lands and later intermarriage between their descendants, Thomas Ford was likely this transported servant.

## Land Survey: "Fordstone" (17 November 1659)

The earliest confirmed record placing Thomas in Anne Arundel County is the survey of the tract called "Fordstone" on 17 November 1659. The land was located near Herring Creek.

## Death and Estate

Thomas Ford is presumed to have died in Anne Arundel County in or about October 1682, when his estate was inventoried. He married Eliza (surname unknown).

## Son James Ford's Will (1702)

James Ford (1666-16 February 1703) of Herring Creek, Anne Arundel County. Will dated 28 July 1702, proved 16 December 1702. Left to wife Eliza as executrix the "Upper Plantation" during life. To sons John and James and their heirs: 300 acres called "Rockford" on the south side of the Susquehanna.

## Data Discrepancy

The GEDCOM import gives Thomas Ford's birth as 1 November 1629 in Hindon, Wiltshire, England, and death as 7 June 1688. The research findings suggest death circa October 1682 (when estate inventoried), not 1688. The birth date and location of Hindon, Wiltshire remain unverified.

## Confidence

**Moderate Signal.** Land survey date confirmed through Maryland patent records. The connection to the servant "Thos Ford" is plausible but not proven. Estate inventory date provides a terminus ante quem for death. Primary sources: Maryland State Archives patent volumes, Anne Arundel County inventory records.
