# Colonial Records Search: Hale Great Grandmother's Side (Bourne and Monroe Lines)

This audit covers six ancestors on the Hale great grandmother's side, specifically the Bourne, Monroe, Burwell, Meador, and Haile lines in colonial Virginia. Twelve web searches were conducted on 2026-04-06.

---

## 1. William F Bourne Sr (1743, 1836), Knob Fork, Grayson County, Virginia

### Records Found

**Land and Property Records (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Bournes of Grayson County, Virginia](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm). William's grandfather, William Bourne II, obtained a 300 acre land grant in 1719, signed by Lieutenant Governor Alexander Spottswood. William F Bourne Sr and his brothers Stephen and John grew up on this grant. William and his wife Rosa remained in Louisa County for at least the first ten years of their marriage (per tax records), then relocated to Grayson County.

**County Formation and Clerk of Court (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Bournes of Grayson County](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm); [WikiTree: Bourne-405](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Bourne-405). William was instrumental in forming Grayson County. The first County Court was held in his barn on Knob Fork of Elk Creek on May 21, 1793. He was appointed Clerk of the Court and held the position for 17 years.

**Business Interests (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Bournes of Grayson County](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm). William partnered with his son William Bourne III to purchase 100 acres including the Point Hope Furnace and Forge, a gristmill, sawmill, and associated structures. Point Hope Furnace on Peach Bottom Creek was Grayson County's first industry, processing iron ore since before the Revolution.

**Estate Sale (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Bournes of Grayson County](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm). In 1836 the Bournes sold their large estate on Knob Fork to A.M. Young of Iredell County, North Carolina.

**Burial (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [Find a Grave: William Bourne Sr.](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/57997875/william-bourne). William and Rosa are buried where they built their first house on Knob Fork.

### Negative Results
No specific Revolutionary War pension or bounty land warrant application was found for William F Bourne Sr in these searches. No will or probate record was located online, though the 1836 estate sale suggests he disposed of property before death. Further research in Library of Virginia records and Grayson County court order books may yield more.

### Confidence: Strong Signal for land, civic, and business records. No direct military service documentation found.

## 2. Rosamond "Rosa" Jones (1750, 1821), Wife of William F Bourne Sr

### Records Found

**Marriage and Family (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Jones-12381](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Jones-12381); [Bournes of Grayson County](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm). Rosa was born in 1750 and grew up on a large land grant near Fredericksburg, Virginia. Her parents are identified as Thomas and Bette Jones. She had a sister Naomi and three brothers: Minetree Jones Sr., Thomas, and Daniel. She married William Bourne in about 1765 in Hanover County, Virginia.

**Role in Grayson County (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [Bournes of Grayson County](https://www.mccarterfamily.com/mccarterpage/stories/willandrosa_bourne.htm). Rosa was described as the first missionary woman of Grayson County. Court records from 1793 mention the court sitting in "Rosa's cabin."

**Death (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [Find a Grave: Rosamond Bourne](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/57997903/rosamond-bourne). Died 1821, aged approximately 71.

### The Monroe Connection Claim (Speculative)

The claim that Rosamond Jones was a daughter of Colonel Spence Monroe (1727, 1774) and thus a sibling of President James Monroe was **not confirmed** by these searches.

Source: [Geni: Spence Monroe](https://www.geni.com/people/Spence-Monroe/6000000002032335227); [WikiTree: Monroe-431](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Monroe-431); [WeRelate: Spence Monroe](https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Person:Spence_Monroe_(2)). The documented children of Spence Monroe and Elizabeth Jones include: Elizabeth Monroe Buckner, James Monroe (5th President, b. 1758), Spence Monroe Jr. (d. age 1), Andrew Monroe, and Col. Joseph Jones Monroe. No daughter named "Rosamond" appears in any of the genealogical databases consulted for Spence Monroe's family.

Meanwhile, the Bourne family sources identify Rosa's parents as Thomas and Bette Jones, not Spence Monroe. The "Jones" surname in both families may be the source of confusion: Elizabeth Jones was Spence Monroe's wife (and James Monroe's mother), and Rosa's maiden name was also Jones.

**Assessment:** The Monroe connection through Rosamond remains unsubstantiated. The parentage of Thomas and Bette Jones is the version supported by the Bourne family tradition. Further research into Westmoreland County and Louisa County records would be needed to establish or disprove any Jones family link to the Monroe circle.

### Confidence: Speculative for Monroe connection. Moderate Signal for biographical details.

## 3. Lewis Burwell III, Col. (1711, 1756), Fairfield Plantation, Gloucester County, Virginia

### Records Found

**Biographical and Political Career (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Encyclopedia Virginia: Burwell, Lewis (1711 or 1712, 1756)](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/burwell-lewis-1711-or-1712-1756/); [Wikipedia: Burwell family of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwell_family_of_Virginia). Lewis Burwell III was born at Fairfield Plantation, Gloucester County, son of Nathaniel Burwell and Elizabeth Carter. His grandfathers were Lewis Burwell (d. 1710) and Robert "King" Carter.

Educated at Eton College (1722, 1729) and Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge. Elected to the House of Burgesses from Gloucester County in 1742. Appointed to the Governor's Council on February 10, 1743, by King George II. Served as acting Governor of Virginia from November 1750 to November 1751, between the death of Thomas Lee and the arrival of Lt. Governor Robert Dinwiddie.

**Marriage and Children (Strong Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Burwell-18](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Burwell-18); [Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/burwell-lewis-1711-or-1712-1756/). Married Mary Willis in October 1736. They had two or three sons and three daughters before Mary's death in May 1746. Children include: Nathaniel Burwell, Lewis Burwell, Elizabeth Lucy (Burwell) Whiting, Ann Burwell, **Mary (Burwell) Hale**, and Rebecca (Burwell) Ambler. Mary (Burwell) Hale is the daughter who married Capt. Lewis Hale Sr.

**Death and Burial (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Find a Grave: Lewis Burwell](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/18764272/lewis-burwell). Died May 6, 1756, at Fairfield. Buried initially at Fairfield, later reinterred at Abingdon Church, Gloucester County.

### Negative Results
No digitized copy of the actual will text was located in these web searches, though he is well documented in scholarly sources. The Library of Virginia and Gloucester County court records would be the primary repositories.

### Confidence: Strong Signal. Lewis Burwell III is extremely well documented as a colonial official and planter.

## 4. Mary "Polly" Burwell (1744, 1809), Wife of Capt. Lewis Hale Sr

### Records Found

**Parentage and Marriage (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Burwell-114](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Burwell-114); [WikiTree: Haile-16](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-16). Mary Burwell was born in 1744 in Gloucester County, Virginia, daughter of Lewis Burwell III and Mary Willis. She married Lewis Hale (b. November 2, 1742, Lancaster County, Virginia) in 1768.

**Burwell Lineage (Strong Signal)**
Source: [Geni: Captain Lewis Hale I](https://www.geni.com/people/Captain-Lewis-Hale-I/6000000002932537786). Mary was confirmed as a descendant of Robert "King" Carter and Judith Armistead through her Burwell line. This places her firmly in the colonial Virginia gentry.

**Land and Migration (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-16](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-16). Lewis and Mary sold their lands south of the Staunton at the mouth of the Blackwater River during 1778 and 1779, then moved to the New River Valley and settled on Elk Creek (later Grayson County). Lewis Hale was appointed a Justice of Montgomery County in 1786 and was one of the first Justices of Grayson County in 1792.

**Children (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-16](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-16). Eight children: Richard (1769), William (1771), Dudley (1773), Francis (1775), Elizabeth (1777), Lewis II (1781), Mary (1782), and Stephen (1783).

**Lewis Hale's Military Service (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-16](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-16). Lewis Hale was a Revolutionary soldier in 1781. He died in Grayson County in 1802.

### Negative Results
No independent primary source document (deed, will, church record) linking Mary directly to Lewis Burwell III was located online. The connection is widely repeated in genealogical databases but the documentary basis for it is not cited in those entries. Gloucester County parish registers (if surviving) and Burwell family papers would be the next research targets.

### Confidence: Moderate Signal. The Burwell connection is consistently reported but primary documentation was not located in this search.

## 5. Clara Adara Meador (c. 1712/1715, 1757), Wife of Francis Edward Hale, Essex County, Virginia

### Records Found

**Father's Will (Strong Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Meador-32](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Meador-32); [WikiTree: Meador-31](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Meador-31); [WeRelate: Richard Meador and Ann Moss](https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Family:Richard_Meador_and_Ann_Moss_(4)). Richard Meador wrote his will in Essex County on December 12, 1715, and it was probated on May 15, 1716. In the will he left his wife the "plantation whereon I now live during life and then the elderest son William." He left his young son Richard the "land where William Borne now lives, that is to say after sd William and Hester his Wife deceased the sd land being formerly in the possession of my Grandfather." If the son Richard died without issue, the land would pass to "my youngest dau Addra."

**Name Variants (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Meador-31](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Meador-31). Adara's name appears variously as Clara Adara, simply Adara, Clara, and "Addra" (the form used in her father's will). The date December 12, 1715 is the date of her father's will, which some sources have confused with her birth date.

**Parentage (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [Ancestry: Clara Adara Meador](https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/clara-adara-meador-24-1g457t). Parents identified as Richard Meador and Anne Moss of Essex County.

**Meador Family Colonial Context (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [FamilySearch Catalog: Our Meador Families in Colonial America](https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog/168483); [Genealogy Magazine: Overlooked Facts About the Colonial Meadors](https://www.genealogymagazine.com/overlooked-facts-about-the-colonial-meadors-and-beyond/). The Meador family traces to Ambrose Meador, who immigrated from England to Warrisquicke (later Isle of Wight) County before 1636. John Meador Sr. (b. 1658, d. 1721) was politically powerful in Essex County and benefitted from grants along the Rappahannock River. Victor P. Meador published "Our Meador Families in Colonial America" (Independence, MO, 1983), covering records from Isle of Wight, Lancaster, Old Rappahannock, Richmond, Essex, and Caroline Counties.

### Negative Results
No marriage record for Clara/Adara Meador and Francis Hale was found. The Essex County records referenced in the will text suggest further research at the Library of Virginia is warranted. Her death date of 1757 and death location (Bedford, Virginia) are found in secondary sources but no primary document was located.

### Confidence: Strong Signal for her father's will naming her. Moderate Signal for the rest of her biography.

## 6. Dr. John Richard Haile (c. 1670/1675, 1744), Essex County, Virginia

### Records Found

**Land Deed from Father (Strong Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-21](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-21); [WikiTree: Haile-22](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-22). In 1696, Richard Haile deeded 120 acres to his son John Haile "for love and affection" in Essex County, Virginia. The deed was recorded November 22, 1697. Richard Haile (the father) appears in Old Rappahannock records as early as 1671 and died c. 1702/1703, when Old Rappahannock County became Essex County.

**Marriage (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-21](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-21). John married Mary Fullerton in 1699 in South Farnham Parish, Essex County, Colony of Virginia.

**Medical Practice (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-21](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-21); [Geni: Dr. John Haile](https://www.geni.com/people/Dr-John-Haile/296374875810004868). Estate records from Henry Crittendon and John Edmondson contain receipts for services paid to "Dr. John Haile," supporting his identification as a medical practitioner.

**Will and Probate (Strong Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-21](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-21); [The Ancestral Clark](https://vtcrewcat.wordpress.com/2014/07/04/all-hale-the-new-score-the-real-family-story-of-one-of-graysons-forefathers/comment-page-1/). John's last will and testament was dated August 17, 1744, and recorded (probated) October 16, 1744, in Essex County. Children named in the will: Francis, John, Thomas, Benjamin, Mary Edmondson, and Susanna Allen. His widow Mary and son John were appointed executors. Securities were William Tyler and John Ferguson, with a bond of 1,000 pounds sterling.

**Estate Inventory (Strong Signal)**
Source: [WikiTree: Haile-21](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-21). The inventory and appraisal was made by John Ferguson, James Allen, and William Dix on October 16, 1744. He owned: 2 cows and calves, 2 cows and yearlings, 2 barren cows, a steer, bull and yearling, a parcel of hogs, 9 sheep, 1 horse and saddle, a horse collar, and 12 geese.

**Son Francis Hale (Moderate Signal)**
Source: [Colonial Settlers of MD and VA](https://www.colonial-settlers-md-va.us/getperson.php?personID=I32001&tree=Tree1); [WikiTree: Hale-880](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hale-880). After his father's will was probated in 1744, Francis probably moved from Essex County to Caroline County, then eventually settled in Bedford County, where he died c. 1780. Francis married Clara Adara Meador (see section 5 above).

### Negative Results
The "Dr." prefix is supported only by estate receipts from third parties, not by any medical license or formal documentation. Birth year estimates vary (1670 to 1676). No church baptism or birth record was found.

### Confidence: Strong Signal for will, probate, estate inventory, and land deed. Moderate Signal for medical practice. The earliest documented Hale ancestor in this line is actually Richard Haile (John's father), who appears in Old Rappahannock records from 1671.

### Key Findings

1. **Monroe Connection Unsubstantiated.** The claim that Rosamond Jones was a daughter of Colonel Spence Monroe and thus a sibling of President James Monroe is not supported by any source found. Spence Monroe's documented children do not include a Rosamond. The Bourne family tradition names her parents as Thomas and Bette Jones. The shared "Jones" surname (Elizabeth Jones was Spence Monroe's wife) may be the origin of this conflation.

2. **Burwell Line Well Documented.** Lewis Burwell III is one of the best documented figures in this tree: Governor's Council member, acting Governor, Eton and Cambridge educated. His daughter Mary's marriage to Lewis Hale connects the Hale family to the colonial Virginia elite, though the specific primary document linking Mary to Burwell III was not found in this search.

3. **Haile Line Goes Back to 1671.** Dr. John Haile's father, Richard Haile, appears in Old Rappahannock records from 1671 and deeded land to John in 1696. This pushes the documented Hale presence in Virginia to the late 17th century. John's 1744 will and estate inventory are the strongest primary source documents found in this search.

4. **Meador Will Is a Primary Source.** Richard Meador's December 12, 1715, will naming his daughter "Addra" is the key document linking Clara Adara Meador to the Essex County Meadors. The will text is quoted in multiple secondary sources and appears to survive in Essex County records.

5. **Grayson County Convergence.** Both the Bourne and Hale families converged in Grayson County in the late 18th century. William Bourne held the first court in his barn (1793); Lewis Hale was one of the first justices (1792). This geographic proximity explains the later intermarriage of the families.

### Recommended Next Steps

- Search Library of Virginia digital collections for Lewis Burwell III's will (Gloucester County, 1756).
- Search Westmoreland County and Louisa County records for any Jones family connection to the Monroe circle.
- Obtain the full text of Richard Meador's 1715 Essex County will.
- Obtain the full text of Dr. John Haile's 1744 Essex County will.
- Investigate whether Victor P. Meador's 1983 book "Our Meador Families in Colonial America" is available through interlibrary loan.
- Search Revolutionary War pension records more specifically for William Bourne of Grayson County.
