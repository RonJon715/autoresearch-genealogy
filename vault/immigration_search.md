### 3. Martin Hedrich Sr. (b. 1920): 1950 Arrival Confirmed via Obituary

**Confidence: Strong Signal (obituary primary source)**

Source: Martin Hedrich Obituary, Legacy.com, Crystal Lake, Illinois, May 2015. "Martin was born in Liebling, Romania and by the grace of God was able to bring his family through Austria to the United States in 1950. This was done through the effort of his uncles, George, John and Jake Heisler, who were brothers of his mother and lived in the Crystal Lake area."

This confirms the 1950 arrival year and the route through Austria (consistent with DP camp residency). Martin Hedrich Sr. (b. 1920) is distinct from Martin Hedrich (b. 1911) in the vault; both were from Liebling. The Heisler family connection in Crystal Lake provided the sponsorship required under the Displaced Persons Act of 1948.

Note: Martin Hedrich Sr. (b. 1920) married Rosina Bartolf (b. 2 Aug 1922, Liebling, Romania; d. 26 Apr 2018). They came to the US together in 1950. (Source: Rosina Hedrich obituary, Davenport Family Funeral Homes)

### Key Gap: No Passenger Manifest Found for c. 1950 DP Immigration

The primary immigrant ancestors (Friedrich Hedrich b. 1903, Philippina Kind Hedrich b. 1906, Friedrich Hedrich b. 1927, Maria Koller b. 1929, Elisabetha Koller b. 1906) all arrived in the United States c. 1950 as post-WWII displaced persons from the Romanian Banat. No passenger manifest or ship name has been identified for any of these individuals.

Displaced persons typically traveled on U.S. Army transports (USAT/USNS) from Bremerhaven, Germany to New York. Known ships in this service included the USNS General C.C. Ballou, USAT General W.G. Haan, USAT General Black, and others. Passenger manifests for these vessels are held by the National Archives (NARA) as part of the New York Passenger Lists, 1820-1957 collection.

## Recommendations for Further Research

### High Priority

1. **Retrieve naturalization petitions for Friedrich Hedrich (b. 1927) and Maria Koller (b. 1929).** These post-1906 federal naturalization records (National Archives at Chicago, RG 21) are the single most valuable records for this family. They would contain: exact birthplace (town), birth date, arrival date, port of entry, ship name, occupation, physical description, current address, and witnesses. Access options:
   - FamilySearch: Browse the collection "Illinois, Northern District Petitions for Naturalization, 1906-1991" (free, requires account login). Use the petition numbers from the Ancestry.com index hints.
   - National Archives at Chicago: Request copies by mail or visit in person. (700 W. Adams Street, Chicago, IL 60661)
   - Ancestry.com: Access the full images in "Illinois, U.S., Federal Naturalization Records, 1856-1991" (subscription required).

2. **Search the Arolsen Archives online portal (arolsen-archives.org) for DP camp registration cards.** Search for: Friedrich Hedrich, Philippina Hedrich, Friedrich Hedrich Jr., Maria Koller, Elisabetha Koller. DP camp registration cards typically contain: full name, date of birth, place of birth, nationality, religion, occupation, family members, camp name, and emigration destination. The Arolsen Archives are free and open to all.

3. **Request USCIS Genealogy Program records for Friedrich Hedrich (b. 1903) and Friedrich Hedrich (b. 1927).** The USCIS Genealogy Program (uscis.gov/records/genealogy) can provide:
   - Immigrant Visa files (A-Files) for arrivals after 1 Apr 1944
   - Naturalization Certificate Files (C-Files) for naturalizations after 1906
   - Cost: $65 per index search; additional fee for file copies
   - Submit Form G-1041 online or by mail

### Medium Priority

4. **Obtain Philippina Hedrich's Social Security Application (SS-5).** Request from the Social Security Administration using Form SSA-711. Cost: $16 for a deceased individual. The SS-5 would show her date of application, birth date, birthplace, parents' names, and employer at time of application. This would help establish a firm arrival date.

5. **Contact the Lieblinger Verein Chicago (lieblingerchicago.com).** This community organization of ethnic Germans from Liebling may hold: membership rolls, oral history recordings, photographs, and community histories that name specific families and arrival dates. They reportedly maintain a video documenting the 1944 trek out of Liebling.

6. **Consult the Liebling Ortsfamilienbuch.** Johann Mohler, *Ortssippenbuch Liebling im Banat* (Altshausen, 1979), 836 pages, covering 6,303 families. This published parish family book would provide verified vital data (baptisms, marriages, deaths) for the Hedrich, Koller, and Kind families in Liebling. Access options:
   - St. Louis County Library, History and Genealogy Department: offers up to three surname lookups by email with up to thirty pages scanned at no charge.
   - FamilySearch Library (Salt Lake City): holds a copy.
   - Also: Adam Arnold, *Lieblinger Familienbuch* (1994; 2001 CD-ROM edition).

7. **Search the Dreyer Database directly.** The rootsweb-hosted pages returned 403 errors during this search session. Try again at a later date, or contact David Dreyer directly (2325 Donegal Street, South San Francisco, CA 94080) for copies of original records. The database covers 1896 to 1938 only, so it documents earlier chain migration, not the post-WWII wave.

### Lower Priority

8. **Search Ancestry.com New York Passenger Lists, 1820-1957 for displaced persons arrivals c. 1949-1952.** Search by name variants: Hedrich, Hederich, Hetrich. Filter by arrival year range 1948-1952. Also try searching by ship names common in the DP transport service (General Ballou, General Haan, General Black, General Muir, General Stuart).

9. **Request International Refugee Organization (IRO) records from the Truman Library.** The Harry S. Truman Library holds IRO records including a passenger list for the first refugee shipload (USAT General Black, Bremerhaven to New York, 21-30 Oct 1948). Additional IRO press releases and passenger documentation cover February 1949 through November 1950.

10. **Check Georg Koller (b. 1902, d. 1949) death records.** Georg died in 1949, before the main wave of DP immigration. Determining whether he died in Liebling, in a DP camp, or in transit would clarify the family's displacement timeline. The Arolsen Archives may hold relevant records if he died in a DP camp.
