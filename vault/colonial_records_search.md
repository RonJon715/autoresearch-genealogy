# Colonial Records Search

Targeted search of primary source records for five key ancestors with deep Virginia and Maryland colonial roots. Conducted 2026-04-06.

---

## 1. William Monroe (1666, 1737), Westmoreland County, Virginia

### Records Found

**Will of William Monroe, probated 1737, Westmoreland County, Virginia.**
The will appears in Westmoreland County, Virginia Wills, 1654, 1800, p. 102. It names four sons, three daughters, and several grandchildren. He left a plantation in King George County, Virginia to his son Thomas. Source: [Ancestry, Westmoreland County, Virginia Wills 1654-1800](https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/4900/); [Colonial Settlers of Southern MD and VA's Northern Neck](https://www.colonial-settlers-md-va.us/getperson.php?personID=I025600&tree=tree1).

**Major Andrew Monroe, land patent, 8 June 1650, Northumberland County, Virginia.**
Andrew Monroe received a 200 acre "Head Rights" land grant dated 8 June 1650 in Northumberland County, on Monroe Creek. Andrew had fought under General Sir George Munro at the Battle of Preston (17 August 1648), was taken prisoner, and was banished to Virginia. He is William Monroe's father. Source: [WikiTree, Andrew Monroe](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Monroe-377); [H600 Project, Monroe families of Virginia](https://h600.org/wiki/B09-MonroeOfVirginia).

**Marriage record reference.**
William Monroe married Margaret Bowcock before 22 March 1689 in the Northern Neck of Virginia. Source: [WikiTree, William Monroe I](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Monroe-461).

**Historical note.** William Monroe is an ancestor of President James Monroe.

### Records Not Found

- No original scan of the 1737 will was located online; the Ancestry collection entry confirms its existence at page 102, but a digitized image was not freely accessible.
- No land patents in William Monroe's own name were found; land holdings appear documented only through the will's bequests.

### Confidence

**Strong Signal.** The will is documented in a published county will collection with a specific page reference. The Andrew Monroe land patent is corroborated across multiple genealogical databases and appears in the "Cavaliers and Pioneers" abstracts of Virginia land patents.

## 2. Emanuel Ford Faulkner Sr. (1718, 1798), Queen Anne's County, Maryland to Iredell County, North Carolina

### Records Found

**Lord Granville land grant, 27 November 1760.**
Thomas Child, agent for Lord Granville acting for King George II, granted 695 acres of vacant land to Emanuel Falkner, lying on Sandy Creek and John Dickerson's line (Granville County, North Carolina). He later held a 795 acre plantation with slaves on Sandy Creek. Source: [FamilySearch Ancestors, Emanuel Ford Faulkner Sr.](https://ancestors.familysearch.org/en/LBS4-V3M/emanuel-ford-faulkner-sr-1718-1798).

**Will of Emanuel Forkner, Iredell County, North Carolina (probated c.1798).**
The will reads: "Be it known to all men that I, Emanuel Forkner, Of Iredell County in the state of North Carolina, being in perfect mind and sound memory...I give unto my beloved wife, Elizabeth Forkner, My land and three Negroes, Eliza, Carry and Molly, and all estate of whatsoever kind I am possessed with until her death." He effectively disinherited all but two of his ten children. Elizabeth died later in the same year, 1798. Source: [Geni, Emmanuel Ford Faulkner Sr.](https://www.geni.com/people/Emmanuel-Faulkner-Sr/6000000010412765297); [WikiTree, Emanuel Ford Faulkner](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1843).

**Migration path documented.**
Born 1718 on the family plantation in Queen Anne's County, Maryland. Moved to Granville County, NC (later Warren County after county divisions), then migrated to Iredell County between 1790 and 1798. Described as illiterate. Source: [FamilySearch Ancestors](https://ancestors.familysearch.org/en/LBS4-V3M/emanuel-ford-faulkner-sr-1718-1798).

### Records Not Found

- No Iredell County estate file was located in the [NC Genealogical Society Iredell County Estate Index](https://www.ncgenealogy.org/north-carolina-estate-files-iredell-county-index/). The will text is cited in secondary sources but the original probate book and page number were not found.
- No Queen Anne's County, Maryland birth or christening record was found.

### Confidence

**Moderate Signal.** The will text is widely quoted but no specific probate book/page citation has been located. The Granville land grant is documented with a date and acreage. Spelling variants (Falkner, Forkner, Fortner, Falconer) complicate record searches.

## 3. Capt. Lewis Hail Sr. (c.1742, 1802), Grayson County, Virginia

### Records Found

**DAR Patriot Ancestor designation.**
Lewis Haile Sr. is listed as a DAR Patriot Ancestor with number A049270. Note: the user provided DAR Patriot #442151, but online sources consistently list A049270. This discrepancy should be verified against official DAR records. Source: [WikiTree, Lewis B. Haile Sr.](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-16).

**Revolutionary War service.**
Lewis Hale served in the Virginia militia during the Revolutionary War. He is believed to have fought at the Battle of King's Mountain (7 October 1780). He was appointed a Justice of Montgomery County in 1786 and was one of the first Justices of Grayson County in 1792. Source: [WikiTree, Lewis B. Haile Sr.](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Haile-16); [Geni, Captain Lewis Hale I](https://www.geni.com/people/Captain-Lewis-Hale-I/6000000002932537786); [Find a Grave, Lewis Hale](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/11400208/lewis-hale).

**Family details.**
Married Mary Burwell in 1768. Lewis and Mary migrated from Franklin County, Virginia toward Kentucky but settled in Elk Creek, Grayson County, Virginia. He died 2 July 1802, buried near his home. Children: Richard, William, Dudley, Francis, Lewis, Stephen, Elizabeth, and Mary.

### Records Not Found

- No original muster roll or pay record from King's Mountain was found for Lewis Hale/Haile/Hail in online sources. The [King's Mountain Patriot Roster](https://sites.rootsweb.com/~scyork/RevWar/KingsMtnRoster.html) and the [Carolana Known Patriots at King's Mountain PDF](https://www.carolana.com/SC/Revolution/Known_Patriots_at_Kings_Mountain.pdf) were checked; his name was not confirmed on these rosters directly in the search results.
- No pension application was found (consistent with him dying in 1802, before the major pension acts of 1818 and 1832).
- The DAR number discrepancy (#442151 vs. A049270) was not resolved.

### Confidence

**Moderate Signal.** The DAR designation confirms patriotic service but the specific Battle of King's Mountain participation uses hedging language ("it is believed"). The Justice of the Peace appointments in Montgomery and Grayson Counties are well documented civic records. Surname spelling (Hail, Hale, Haile) adds complexity.

## 4. John Thomas Faulkner Sr. (c.1628, 1708), London to Queen Anne's County, Maryland

### Records Found

**Immigration record, 22 January 1665.**
John Faulkner Sr. (also Forkner, Fortner, Falkner, Falconer) arrived on the ship "Agreement" on 22 January 1665 in Kent County, Colony of Maryland. He brought his wife Elizabeth, sons Thomas, Francis, and John Jr., and two servants. He paid 300 pounds of tobacco for their passage, indicating wealth (occupation listed as draper, a cloth merchant). Source: [Geni, John Faulkner Sr.](https://www.geni.com/people/John-Faulkner-Sr/6000000010412760095); [WikiTree, John Faulkner Sr.](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Faulkner-1146).

**Maryland colonial land patent system.**
Maryland Patent Liber records covering this period are available through [MDLandRec.net](https://mdlandrec.net) (free registration required) and the [Maryland State Archives land patents guide](https://guide.msa.maryland.gov/pages/viewer.aspx?page=land-patents). An Early Settlers Index for 1633, 1685 is searchable by name. These resources were identified but not searched directly (they require registration or in person access).

### Records Not Found

- No specific land patent (liber, folio, acreage) was located for John Faulkner in online search results. The Maryland State Archives Early Settlers Index (1633, 1685) should contain his headright claim given the 1665 arrival with family and servants.
- No probate record was found. [Maryland colonial wills at the Maryland State Archives](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/stagsere/se1/se4/000000/html/index.html) and [Findmypast Maryland Colonial Probate Records 1634-1777](https://search.findmypast.com/search-world-records/maryland-index-to-colonial-probate-records-1634-1777) were identified as resources but no specific entry was returned in search results.
- The connection to Queen Anne's County (formed 1706 from portions of Kent, Dorchester, and Talbot Counties) was not documented with a specific record. His initial settlement was Kent County; the family may have remained in the same location as the county boundary changed.

### Confidence

**Moderate Signal.** The 1665 immigration on the "Agreement" is well attested across multiple sources. However, no primary source land patent or probate record has been located online. The Maryland State Archives likely holds both but requires direct searching of their digitized Patent Liber records. The death date of 1708 is found in secondary genealogical sources only.

## 5. Houston Ambrose (c.1645), Anne Arundel County, Maryland

### Records Found

No records specific to a person named "Houston Ambrose" born c.1645 were found in any online source.

### Records Not Found

- Searched "Houston Ambrose 1645 Anne Arundel County Maryland colonial records." No results returned for this specific individual.
- Anne Arundel County was not formally created until 29 April 1650, meaning records from 1645 would fall under the Province of Maryland general records.
- The [FamilySearch Anne Arundel County Maryland Genealogy page](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Anne_Arundel_County,_Maryland_Genealogy) lists available record sets but no "Houston Ambrose" was indexed.
- The [Maryland State Archives colonial wills](https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/stagsere/se1/se4/000000/html/index.html) and [Findmypast Maryland Colonial Probate Index 1634-1777](https://search.findmypast.com/search-world-records/maryland-index-to-colonial-probate-records-1634-1777) were identified as potential resources but returned no results.
- A related search returned an "Ambrose Nelson Sr. (abt.1645, abt.1722)" in Anne Arundel County who appeared in a 1677 Record of Land Patents. This may or may not be related; the name overlap is noted for follow up.

### Confidence

**Speculative.** No primary or secondary source was found for a person named Houston Ambrose in colonial Maryland. The name may be a patronymic confusion, a transcription error, or may require searching under variant spellings. The vault already contains an "Ambrose" folder that should be cross referenced for additional clues.
