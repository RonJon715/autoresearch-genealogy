## Biography

William Monroe (b. 1666, d. 30 March 1737) of Westmoreland County, Virginia. Son of Major Andrew Monroe (d. 1668), who immigrated from Scotland (son of David Munro of Katewell) and patented 200 acres in Northumberland County in 1650 as a "Head Right." Andrew Monroe built Monrovia (Monroe Hall) on Monroe Creek near present day Colonial Beach, Westmoreland County. William's will (probated 1737, Westmoreland County) names sons Thomas, George, William, and Andrew, plus daughters Sarah Stone and Mary Stone, and grandson Spence (son of Andrew). The grandson Spence Monroe (1727-1774) was the father of President James Monroe. (Sources: WikiTree Monroe-461; colonial-settlers-md-va.us; Fothergill, *Wills of Westmoreland County, Virginia*)

**Local History Source**: William Monroe's will (1737) is documented in *Westmoreland County, Virginia Wills 1654-1800*, p. 102. The will details bequests to: son William (named executor); grandson George (son of William); son Thomas (received land); grandson Thomas Munroe; grandsons Spence and his brother Andrew Munroe; granddaughter Jane Munroe; son George; grandson Daniel Payne; Jeane Payne; daughters Sarah Stone and Mary Stone; grandsons William Payne and William Stone; and son Andrew Munroe's children Spence, Andrew, and Jane. (Strong Signal; published will transcription, primary source transcribed in secondary compilation)

William is listed in documents as "Gent," indicating gentleman status. His father Andrew Monroe emigrated from Scotland to America in 1650, belonged to an ancient highland clan, and was Captain in the service of King Charles I of England. Andrew received his first grant of land June 8, 1650, in Northumberland County, Virginia. (Moderate Signal; *William and Mary College Quarterly Historical Magazine*, Vols. 15-16; *The American Genealogist*, Vol. 46, p. 183; Lyon G. Tyler, "James Monroe," *William and Mary Quarterly*, Vol. 4, No. 4, Apr. 1896, pp. 272-275)

After the death of his first wife Margaret (Bowcock), William married Susanna White per *Colonial Virginians and Their Maryland Relatives* by Norman Tucker. (Moderate Signal; secondary source)
