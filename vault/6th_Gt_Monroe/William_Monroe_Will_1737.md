# Will of William Monroe (1737)

## Source

Will recorded 30 March 1737 in Westmoreland County, Virginia. Referenced in Fothergill, *Wills of Westmoreland County, Virginia, 1654, 1800* (1925; reprinted 1990).

## Summary

William Monroe's will, probated at Westmoreland County, left the following bequests:

**Plantation in King George County**: To son Thomas
**Grandson Spence and brother Andrew Munroe**: Mentioned
**Granddaughter Jane Munroe**: Mentioned
**Son George**: Mentioned
**Grandson George, son of William Munroe**: Mentioned
**Grandson Daniel Payne**: Mentioned
**Jeane Payne**: Mentioned
**Daughters**: Sarah Stone and Mary Stone
**Grandsons**: William Payne and William Stone
**Son Andrew Munroe's children**: Spence, Andrew, and Jane
**Executor**: Son William

## Monroe Family Land Background

Major Andrew Monroe, son of David Munro of Katewell (Scotland), patented a large tract of land in Washington Parish, Westmoreland County, Virginia in 1650. He built Monrovia (Monroe Hall) on Monroe Creek, a tributary of the Potomac River near present day Colonial Beach. Andrew Monroe died 1668, leaving six heirs including William.

The will confirms William Monroe held lands in both Westmoreland and King George Counties. His grandson Spence Monroe (1727, 1774) was the father of President James Monroe.

## Calendar Note

Date recorded as 30 March 1737. Under the Old Style (Julian) calendar used in Virginia until 1752, the legal new year began on March 25, so this date would be 30 March 1736/37 in dual dating notation. The Gregorian (New Style) date is 10 April 1737.
