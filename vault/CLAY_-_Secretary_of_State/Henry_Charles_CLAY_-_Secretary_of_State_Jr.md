## Biography

Henry Clay "The Elder" (3 August 1672, 3 August 1760) of Dale Parish, Chesterfield (originally Henrico) County, Virginia. Son of Charles Clay and Hannah Wilson. Married Mary Mitchell in 1709 in Henrico County. He was an Indian trader ca. 1711-1712, traveling "as far beyond Carolina as it was to it." He owned large tracts of land in Henrico, Goochland, Chesterfield, and Cumberland Counties. His will (signed 28 March 1749, probated September 1760 at Chesterfield Court) left land on Deep Creek in Henrico County to son William and a plantation plus 200 acres at "Let Alone" in Goochland County to son Henry. In 1726, he witnessed Ralph Blankenship's will. His tombstone reads: "In Memory of Henry Clay Who died at dinner with his children and grandchildren at an annual festival given them on August 3, 1760." He was the great grandfather of statesman Henry Clay (1777-1852). (Sources: WikiTree Clay-84; *Adventurers of Purse and Person, Virginia 1607-1624/5*, 3rd ed., pp. 193-197; Filson Club Publications No. 14, *The Clay Family*, 1899)

## Data Discrepancies

- GEDCOM lists Father as Unknown. Father was Charles Clay (b. ca. 1640, d. ca. 1696), per WikiTree Clay-84 and published genealogies. Confidence: Moderate Signal.
- GEDCOM name "Henry Charles CLAY - Secretary of State Jr" is misleading. Henry Clay (1672-1760) was not a Secretary of State; his great grandson Henry Clay (1777-1852) was. The "Jr" designation likely distinguished him from his father Charles Clay in the community.
