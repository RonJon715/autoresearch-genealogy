## Biography

According to James H. Miller, *History of Summers County, West Virginia* (Hinton, WV: J.H. Miller, 1908), pp. 403-404: "Probably the first settler by the name of Meador within our territory was Josiah Meador." The original family name was "Meadows," but many descendants now use "Meador." Josiah and Jacob Meadows, two of a family, came to the Bluestone/Jumping Branch region after the surrender of Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown (1781), arriving about 1780 to 1785. (Moderate Signal; secondary source, county history)

Josiah's wife was Juda Lilly. After his death, she married John Woodrum. (Moderate Signal; Miller 1908)

Miller records that Josiah enlisted in the early spring of 1778 under Captain Joseph Renfroe and marched to Jarrett's Fort on Wolf Creek (now in Summers County). Upon expiration of his enlistment, he re-enlisted in the company of Captain Isaac Taylor and marched through the Holstein country, then with Colonel George Rogers Clark's expedition through the Illinois Country, and was later with the American Army guarding British prisoners captured at Yorktown. (Moderate Signal; Miller 1908)

Josiah was "a fighting missionary Baptist minister," locating on Bluestone River at the mouth of Little Bluestone. He organized the first Baptist church in the section, the Bluestone Baptist Church, organized in 1798 in his house on Little Bluestone River, described as "a double log cabin with a dirt floor." (Moderate Signal; Miller 1908)

**DISCREPANCY**: The vault lists his wife as [[Lavina_LILLY_MOODY_Meador]], while Miller (1908) identifies his wife as "Juda Lilly." These may be the same person (with "Lavina" being an alternate name or middle name), or they may represent confusion between generations. This warrants further research.

**Note**: The Meador/Meadows and Lilly families became closely allied by marriage. Miller notes: "If a candidate for office had the support of the Lilly and Meador families, he was sure of election."
